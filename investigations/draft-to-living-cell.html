Executable atlas · A meta-modeler's guide to the cellular interface

From Draft to Living Cell

A cellular interface is not just a modeler's simplification — it is a testable hypothesis about which interactions matter, and staying alive is what makes the boundary real. A cell is a self-maintaining, adaptive agent exactly to the extent that it holds its exchange variables inside a viability bound; crossing that bound is what forces a model — and the biology it represents — to be cut open and re-described at another grain. This investigation follows the paper's own arc across 9 studies, each written first as an inert typed draft and then compiled into a running executable that preserves the interface exactly, from the interface itself through where it holds, where it fails, and how it is rebuilt at another level. The flagship exhibit is Disintegration (Fig 6) — a viability collapse that can be played step by step, plus the same metabolic interface realized at three grains — coarse, kinetic, and real COBRApy flux-balance — behind ports that never move.

The reading holds, and it runs, across all 9 studies in the paper's order. The interface is a testable hypothesis, not just a convenience: cellular-interface (Fig 4) compiles a typed contract into a bounded, goal-directed cell, and home to law 1 conformance — the one concrete impostor handler in this codebase (a non-conforming metabolism that renames/drops ports) is rejected by the compiler at compile time with a CompileError naming the missing ports; that impostor itself lives in the metabolism/Fig 6 code, cross-referenced here from disintegration. Cell-environment coupling (Fig 5) closes a genuine sense/act loop over a real diffusing spatial field — niche construction, not scripted (law 2). Cell-cell coupling (no figure of its own) shows that composing two cell interfaces over one shared nutrient store couples constraint, not just state: competition starves the weaker cell while the stronger persists; a different handler over the same interface (cross-feeding) keeps both alive (law 4). The flagship is Disintegration (Fig 6): playable — a scripted thermal shock collapses viability, halts viability-gated metabolism, and turns biomass into molecular debris, watchable step by step in the Composite Explorer — and structural: the same metabolic-exchange interface is realized at three grains (a coarse lumped yield, a saturating Michaelis-Menten kinetic, and real COBRApy flux-balance optimization over e_coli_core) behind ports that never move — the cell-to-molecular- network grain-swap that carries coarse, kinetic, and real-FBA behavior behind one interface (law 4). Molecular interfaces (Fig 7) drops one level further: an F1Fo ATP-synthase mechanism drives all four physical channels — chemical, electrical, mechanical, thermal — from one coupled proton flux, honoring the four-channel contract — an example of the kind of molecular grain disintegration drops into. Biomolecular complementarity (Fig 8) nests six place-graph levels deep with a coupled gene- expression cascade wired to the deepest leaf, and the interface survives that nesting — law 2's deepest test in this codebase; complementarity/selectivity is narrated as the paper's organizing idea, but hierarchical assembly is what the executable demonstrates, not a binding-affinity mechanism. Autopoiesis (Fig 9) compiles metabolism, containment, and replication into mutual closure toward a minimal cell — law 4's second grain-swap home — but this is the closure pattern demonstrated qualitatively, not a validated autopoietic system. Growth and division (Fig 10a,b) is the sharpest structural result: an autocatalytic, energy- coupled growth process drives the cell's own DNA past a threshold, and crossing it fires a genuine place-graph rewrite — one cell node becomes two, mass conserved (the parent is halved, not duplicated) — law 2' (rewrite conformance) alongside law 2 (interface preserved across the rewrite). Development and evolution (Fig 10c-f) is explicitly caveated: biofilm nesting and port-addition/selection compile and run as event-driven rewrites, and a "Live Topology" pair for each genuinely inserts and removes place-graph nodes, but selection is a single ODE with fixed constants and the "new port" a scripted config ramp — this study's own gate is needs_calibration, and the paper itself calls this transition "an open and substantial challenge." Finally, a capstone assembled by hand (not compiler-emitted) carries the disintegration grain-swap up to a whole cell — coarse, kinetic, and real-FBA metabolism behind one interface give three distinct life histories, and each cell grows, divides once (mass-conserved), and dies on a scripted thermal shock. Introduction

This investigation turns A meta-modeler's guide to the cellular interface into an executable test of the paper's central claim: a cellular interface is simultaneously a modeling choice and a testable biological hypothesis about which interactions govern a cell's fate. Each study is written twice. First as a draft: a typed, unit-bearing interface — a structural contract of port names, types, and wiring, with no committed mechanism. It says what a process couples to, not how. Then it is compiled, which installs one or more conforming mechanisms — mechanistic, rule-based, data-driven, or otherwise — behind the identical ports, so the running simulation preserves the declared interface exactly. This operationalizes the paper's R_L (the process- implementation relation) and the Discussion's semantic-to-executable layer: a draft is the semantic layer, and compilation is the step that gives it an executable one. The compiler's conformance check is structural only: it verifies that a handler supplies every port with a compatible type and wiring, and refuses one that does not. It does not check units, dimensions, invariants, or runtime behavior.

The point is not to assemble one monolithic cell model. It is to test whether composition is an ongoing practice rather than a final architecture: connect two interfaces where their assumptions hold (cell-environment and cell-cell coupling), cut the model open at the interface when those assumptions fail and the right level of description drops to molecules (disintegration — the paper's Fig 6, and this investigation's flagship), and coarse-grain a resolved molecular network back into a lumped exchange when organization re-emerges (the same pattern run in reverse, and again in autopoiesis). Underneath every study sits one throughline: viability bounds and minimal agency. A cellular description is valid only while its interface variables sit inside a viability bound; the interface is the locus where ordinary chemical dynamics becomes a self-maintaining, adaptive agent, and losing that boundary is exactly what disintegration demonstrates.

The 9 studies below follow the paper's own order: the cellular interface itself, then where it couples to environment and to other cells, then where it breaks (disintegration) and what it is made of underneath (molecular interfaces, biomolecular complementarity, autopoiesis), then how it is rewritten across time (growth and division, development and evolution). A closing capstone carries the disintegration grain-swap up to one hand-assembled whole cell that grows, divides, and dies; a coverage note confirms that every figure draft in this investigation compiles to a running executable.

  • cell-environment-coupling (Fig 5) — the interface closes into a genuine sense/act loop over a real diffusing spatial field; the cell measurably reshapes the gradient it depends on (niche construction), interface preserved unchanged (law 2).
  • cell-cell-coupling (no figure of its own) — two cells coupled over one shared nutrient interface negotiate viability, not just state: competition starves the weaker cell while the stronger persists; cross-feeding, a different handler over the same interface, keeps both alive (law 4).
  • disintegration (Fig 6, the flagship) — playable: a scripted thermal shock collapses viability, halts viability-gated metabolism, and turns biomass into molecular debris, watchable step by step.   • molecular-interfaces (Fig 7) — an F1Fo ATP-synthase mechanism drives all four physical channels (chemical, electrical, mechanical, thermal) from one coupled proton flux, honoring the interface at a molecular grain of the kind disintegration drops into.
  • biomolecular-complementarity (Fig 8) — a six-level nested place graph (membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, chromosome, chromatin, nucleosome) with a coupled gene-expression cascade wired to the deepest leaf; the interface is preserved through the deepest nesting in this codebase (law 2). Complementarity/selectivity is narrated, not built — hierarchical assembly is demonstrated, binding selectivity is not.
  • autopoiesis (Fig 9) — metabolism, containment, and replication compile into mutual closure toward a minimal cell, a second worked example of law 4''s grain-swap; demonstrates the closure pattern qualitatively, not a validated autopoietic system.
  • growth-and-division (Fig 10a,b) — growth (an autocatalytic, energy-coupled process) drives the cell's own DNA past a threshold; crossing it fires a genuine place-graph rewrite, one cell node becoming two, mass conserved (the parent is halved, not duplicated) — law 2' (rewrite conformance) alongside law 2 (interface preserved across the rewrite).
  • development-and-evolution (Fig 10c-f) — biofilm nesting and port-addition/selection compile and run as event-driven rewrites, including a genuine node-inserting "Live Topology" pair for each; explicitly caveated (gate: needs_calibration) — selection is a single fixed-constant ODE and the new port a scripted ramp, per the paper's own "open and substantial challenge."

Evidence against

  • The handlers are toy-real, not calibrated — plausible constants, not fitted ones — so the dynamics demonstrate the pattern (niche construction, viability collapse, closure, genuine rewrite) rather than a quantitatively validated cell.
  • Conformance is structural only (port names, types, wiring) — the compiler does not check units, dimensions, invariants, or runtime behavior, so a handler can conform structurally while still getting the biology wrong.
  • The capstone whole cell is assembled by hand in the figures' style (wholecell.py), not emitted by the compiler from the studies' own drafts.
  • development-and-evolution's own gate is needs_calibration: its selection and port-addition dynamics are honestly labeled scripted stand-ins, not emergent population or fitness mechanisms.

The Cellular Interface Accepted

Question & findings

Question

Can the cellular boundary be specified as nothing but typed, unit-bearing exchange ports (chemical mol·s⁻¹, mechanical N, electrical C·s⁻¹, thermal J·s⁻¹) plus higher-level variables (growth, shape, objective, viability) — with no committed mechanism — and compiled, by installing one conforming handler, into a bounded, goal-directed cell whose interface is exactly the one declared?

Findings

  • mechanism Installing one conforming handler grows the cell through its interface. Confirmed: shape 1.0 → 4.2.
  • observation The cell takes up chemical across its interface (net negative flux). Confirmed: chemical flux 0.0 → -0.8.
  • mechanism The cell makes progress toward its declared objective as growth proceeds. Confirmed: objective 0.0 → 1.6.
  • mechanism Law 1 (conformance) is machine-checked at compile time: a handler whose declared ports do not match the draft signature is rejected with a CompileError naming the missing ports. The one concrete impostor in the codebase, NonConformingMetabolism, breaks the metabolism interface used by the disintegration/Fig 6 study (not a cellular-interface-specific contract), but it is the mechanical proof that the compiler enforces the same Law-1 structural check for any declared interface, including this one — no cellular-interface-specific impostor exists, and none is claimed here.

Cell–Cell Coupling Accepted

Two cells over one shared-nutrient interface negotiate viability, not just state: under competition the weaker cell (lower uptake capacity) is driven below its viable band while the stronger persists (cell_a viability → 1.0, cell_b → ~0.0001); under cross-feeding, the same coupling interface with a different handler keeps both cells viable (cell_a and cell_b → 1.0) — two handler environments over one interface (Law 4), the draft itself unchanged (Law 2).

COMPETITION STRONGER CELL PERSISTScell_a (vmax 0.8) viability stays at 1.0
SHARED POOL ACTUALLY DEPLETEDenv.nutrient 1.0 → 0.1999... — the shared pool is measurably drawn down, confirming real coupling
Question & findings

Question

When two cell interfaces are coupled through one shared environmental nutrient store, what is coupled is not only state but constraint — does one cell's uptake push the other outside its viable bounds (competition), and can a different handler over the same coupling interface stabilize both (cross-feeding)?

Findings

  • mechanism Coupling two cells through one shared nutrient store couples constraint, not just state: under competition, the cell with lower uptake capacity (cell_b, vmax 0.2) is driven out of its viable band while the stronger cell (cell_a, vmax 0.8) persists. Confirmed: cell_a viability 1.0 → 1.0, cell_b viability 1.0 → 0.0001.
  • mechanism A different handler over the same coupling interface (cross-feeding: symmetric vmax, partial byproduct return to the shared pool) sustains both cells — the constraint negotiated over one shared store is not fixed by the interface, it is set by which handler occupies it. Confirmed: both cell_a and cell_b viability 1.0 → 1.0 under cross-feeding.
  • mechanism The competitive asymmetry and the cross-feeding symmetry live entirely in the handler env layer, never in the draft or the interface — confirming law 4 (handler independence over one coupling interface) and law 2 (interface preserved) simultaneously. Confirmed structurally: interface_of(compete) == interface_of(crossfeed) == interface_of(draft).

Disintegration Accepted

Disintegration is a change in level of description made executable and watchable: playing fig06-disintegration-dynamics, a thermal shock pushes the cell past its viability bound, viability collapses, viability-gated metabolism halts, and biomass decays into molecular debris (Fig 6a). The same metabolic-exchange interface's cell↔molecular-network equivalence carries three grains behind ports that never move — a coarse lumped yield, a saturating kinetic law, and real COBRApy flux-balance on e_coli_core (Fig 6b, Law 4).

BIOMASS TO DEBRISdebris rises 0 → ~4.6 as viability-gated metabolism halts and biomass converts to molecular debris
Question & findings

Question

Fig 6 is disintegration — the change in level of description that fires when a cell crosses its viability bound. Two faces: (a) played through, does the disintegration composite actually show that shift — a thermal shock pushing viability past its bound, viability collapsing, viability-gated metabolism halting, and biomass turning into molecular debris? (b) Is the metabolic-exchange interface at the heart of that same shift realizable at a coarse cell-level grain, an intermediate kinetic grain, and a resolved molecular-network grain (real COBRApy flux-balance optimization) — three grains behind ports that never move?

Findings

  • mechanism Played through, fig06-disintegration-dynamics shows the cell→molecular level shift: a scripted thermal shock pushes the cell past its viability bound and viability collapses. Confirmed: viability 1.0 → ~0 over 20 steps.
  • mechanism Once viability collapses, viability-gated metabolism halts and biomass converts into molecular debris — the level shift is not just a number crossing a threshold, it is mass moving from the cell-level state (biomass) to the molecular-level state (debris). Confirmed: debris 0 → ~4.6.
  • mechanism The linear coarse-grain handler drives the shared metabolic-exchange interface to biomass 4.0. Confirmed: coarse → biomass 4.0 (energy 2.4, entropy 0.8, secretions 1.6).
  • mechanism The Michaelis–Menten handler reaches a distinct endpoint on the identical interface — same ports, different mechanism, different trajectory (law 4), directly asserted by test_fig6_handler_independence. Confirmed: kinetic → biomass 2.667.
  • mechanism The real COBRApy FBA handler — the resolved molecular network, e_coli_core — reaches its own distinct endpoint and shows overflow metabolism: surplus carbon leaves as acetate on the secretions port. This is the third grain of the cell↔molecular-network equivalence. Confirmed: biomass 6.29, secretions 30.4.
  • observation The interface is a real structural contract, not a convention: an impostor handler that breaks the port names/types is rejected at compile time (law 2), independent of which grain is behind the interface. Confirmed: NonConformingMetabolism raises a CompileError naming the missing biomass/energy/entropy/secretions ports.

Question

At the molecular grain, can a single molecular mechanism (F1Fo ATP synthase) be compiled from its draft and run as a PMF-driven rotary catalyst honoring the four physical interface channels (chemical/electrical/mechanical/thermal) and the specialized substrate/cofactor/catalyst/product ports?

Findings

  • mechanism All four physical interface channels move together, driven from the one proton flux the chemical channel sets: chemical (ATP synthesis), electrical (proton current), mechanical (Fo rotary power) and thermal (dissipated remainder) are physically consistent with each other, not four independently fitted outputs. Growth and Division Accepted

09

Development and Evolution Provisional

Development nests individual cells into a biofilm collective behind a preserved, shared extracellular-matrix interface (Law 2), and evolution switches on a new chemical port under a viability-weighted growth term — both compile and run, and a separate "Live Topology" pair for each even performs genuine runtime place-graph node insertion/removal (in the spirit of Law 2′). But — the defining point of this study — none of this is a validated biological result: selection is a single ODE with fixed viability/fitness constants (not real fitness-based population dynamics), the "new port" is a first-order config-driven ramp (not an emergent evolutionary innovation), the continuous-path biofilm state is a pre-declared, non-inserted scalar store, and even the genuine node-inserting Live-Topology composites run on a fixed wall-clock schedule with a hardcoded mutant fitness advantage rather than an emergent, state-driven trigger. The paper itself calls development and evolution "an open and substantial challenge" (line 580); this study demonstrates the pattern, not the phenomenon.

Question & findings

Question

Fig 10c–f asks whether two of biology's hardest structural transitions — development (individual cells nesting into a collective composite, a biofilm, with its own shared interface) and evolution (a new interface port switching on, selected by viability) — can be represented as event-driven, compositional rewrites (Law 2′) that preserve the interface underneath them (Law 2). Two composites answer the first half of that (do the drafts compile to executables that run, behind an unchanged interface?) faithfully. This study asks the harder, second half explicitly: how much of what runs is a genuine developmental/evolutionary mechanism, versus a scripted stand-in — and is honest that the paper itself calls this transition "an open and substantial challenge" (line 580).

Findings

Method & shared caveats

Every figure begins as an inert draft — typed, unit-bearing ports and a behavior contract, no dynamics — and is compiled by installing one conforming mechanism, which preserves the interface exactly (compiler law 2) while giving the figure real dynamics. Readouts below are measured, not asserted: every executable and the whole cell were run and their observables recorded.

Limitations (shared across every study)

  • Two layers, read deliberately: the draft composites are inert (ports + contract, no dynamics) and are rendered in loom; the executable composites run and are shown as dynamics figures. A study's draft render and its executable dynamics are the same interface at two stages, not two different models.
  • The compiler is the binding stage only — it installs a conforming mechanism per draft; it does not fit parameters or invent mechanisms. Conformance is structural (port names, types, wiring), not behavioral — it does not check units, dimensions, invariants, or runtime behavior.
  • The capstone whole cell (see below) is assembled by hand in the figures' style, not emitted by the compiler from any study's drafts. Its division conserves mass (the mother is halved, not doubled) and its death is a scripted thermal shock, not an emergent failure.
  • development-and-evolution is deliberately the most caveated study in the investigation (gate: needs_calibration) — read its own study.yaml before citing its results as more than a pattern demonstration.
