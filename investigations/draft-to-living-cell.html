Introduction

This investigation turns A meta-modeler's guide to the cellular interface into an executable test of compositional modeling. Each figure of the paper is written twice. First as a draft: a typed, unit-bearing biological interface and a behavioral contract, with no committed mechanism — a promise about what a process couples to and does, not how. Then it is compiled, which installs one or more conforming mechanisms — mechanistic, rule-based, data-driven, or otherwise — behind the identical ports, so the running simulation preserves the declared interface exactly.

The point is not to assemble a single monolithic cell model. It is to test whether a biological model can remain open-ended as its mechanisms change, its assumptions fail, and its level of description shifts. Interfaces provide the places where models can be connected, isolated, replaced, or expanded; graph rewrites allow the composition itself to change through events such as growth, division, and disintegration.

The investigation therefore follows the paper's central claim: composition is not the final architecture of a model, but an ongoing way of building one. Across the studies below, we ask whether the same biological specification can support alternative mechanisms, whether those mechanisms can be composed without losing their interface semantics, and whether the resulting system can reorganize as the biology demands. The endpoint is a composed whole cell that grows, divides, loses viability, and changes its own level of description — demonstrating the full arc from interface specification to living, changing simulation.

The thesis, in one figure

One interface, three mechanisms

One metabolism interface — nutrients ⇒ biomass — realized three ways: first-order, Michaelis–Menten, and a real COBRApy LP, run as a closed-loop batch that consumes its own substrate. All three converge to the same final biomass (mass conserved) but with three distinct kinetic signatures — the saturation knee, the capacity plateau, the exponential drawdown. Same interface, conserved mass, different dynamics.

MASS CONSERVED all three converge to biomass = yield·S₀ = 5.0 (mass conserved); coarse/MM/FBA differ in timescale, not endpoint

Evidence for

  • Fig 4 — the cellular interface is a set of typed, unit-bearing ports (chemical mol·s⁻¹, mechanical N, electrical A, thermal W, growth hr⁻¹, viability); a draft declares them, a handler makes a bounded goal-directed cell out of them.
  • Fig 5 — cell–environment coupling closes as a sense/act loop over a shared field store; the executable diffuses a real spatial (map[float]) chemical field.
  • Fig 6 — one metabolism interface (nutrients ⇒ biomass, energy, entropy, secretions) is realized by a coarse process, a saturating-kinetic process, OR real COBRApy FBA — three mechanisms, one interface (handler independence, law 4).
  • Fig 8 — molecular composition is a six-level-deep nested place graph (ECM → membrane → cytoplasm → nucleus → chromosome …) with a coupled gene-expression cascade wired to its leaves; the interface survives the deepest nesting (law 2).
  • Fig 9 — the mutual closure of metabolism, containment, and replication expresses autopoiesis; the same functions appear at coarse, self-organized, and molecular grains.
  • Fig 10 — division, biofilm development, and evolution are compositional rewrites; division is a genuine event-driven graph rewrite (one cell node → two daughter nodes).
  • The whole cell — Figs 5 + 6 + viability + division + disintegration compose into one run that grows, divides, and dies — composition is not a metaphor but a working assembly.

Evidence against

  • The figures are toy-real, not calibrated — handlers use plausible constants, not fitted ones, so the dynamics demonstrate the pattern (ultrasensitivity, closure, division, disintegration) rather than a quantitatively validated cell.
  • Fig 4a (interaction modalities) is illustrative — a vocabulary of ports, not a mechanism — and remains draft-only by design.

The Typed Interface Accepted

A cell can be specified at one level by its interface — typed exchange variables such as chemical flux, force, current, heat, growth, shape, objective, and viability — without committing to the mechanism that realizes those behaviors.

Question

Can the cellular boundary be specified as nothing but a set of typed, unit-bearing exchange ports (chemical, mechanical, electrical, thermal, plus growth rate, shape, objective, viability) with no committed mechanism, and then compiled — by installing one conforming handler — into a running, bounded, goal-directed cell whose interface is exactly the one declared?

Findings

  • mechanism Volume gained is paid for by nutrient consumed (mass balance). Confirmed: volume +3.70 from nutrient −10.00 (yield-bounded).
  • observation Uptake follows a saturating Monod law and depletes the pool. Confirmed: nutrient 10→0 (saturating drawdown).
  • mechanism Viability holds in the tolerance band then collapses past it (Arrhenius). Confirmed: viability 1.0 → ~0 as temperature ramps 37→50 °C past the 42 °C band.

Method & shared caveats

Every figure begins as an inert draft — typed, unit-bearing ports and a behavior contract, no dynamics — and is compiled by installing one conforming mechanism, which preserves the interface exactly (compiler law 2) while giving the figure real dynamics. Readouts below are measured, not asserted: every executable and the whole cell were run and their observables recorded.

Limitations (shared across every study)

  • Two layers, read deliberately: the DRAFT composites are inert (ports + contract, no dynamics) and are rendered in loom; the EXECUTABLE composites run and are shown as dynamics figures. A study's draft render and its executable dynamics are the same interface at two stages, not two different models.
  • The compiler is the binding stage only — it installs a conforming mechanism per draft; it does not fit parameters or invent mechanisms.
