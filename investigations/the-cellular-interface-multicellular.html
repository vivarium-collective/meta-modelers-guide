The Cellular Interface, Multicellular

This is the spatial, multicellular-capable counterpart to draft-to-living-cell: the same nine interface-composition patterns from the paper, realized this time as real 2D spatial frameworks — chiefly a Cellular Potts Model (viva-cpm) coupled to spatio-flux's diffusion-advection and dynamic-FBA processes — rather than lumped ODEs and scripted events. Where draft-to-living-cell asked "can this pattern be authored as an inert typed draft and compiled into a runnable mechanism," this investigation asks "does the pattern survive when the mechanism is a real spatial simulator built by someone else, composed in through one typed interface." The flagship, cell-environment-coupling-spatial, builds and runs exactly one such composition: CpmCellField, a process that reads a spatio-flux nutrient grid at a CPM cell's own footprint, runs one dynamic-FBA step there (e_coli_core, oxygen-capped to force acetate overflow), grows the cell's CPM target volume from the biomass produced, and writes the uptake/secretion back into the shared field as a spatial delta that spatio-flux's DiffusionAdvection then spreads. Over 20 ticks the cell grows roughly 3.4x (32 to 110 CPM lattice pixels, on a 60x60 = 3600-pixel field — it does not come close to filling the lattice), depletes the glucose at its own footprint by up to ~18% before diffusion partially resupplies it, and secretes a spreading acetate plume — niche construction made spatially and metabolically real, not scripted. Three more studies are built and run alongside the flagship — cell-cell coupling (multi-cell field competition), disintegration (structural-integrity release), and growth-and-division. The remaining five (the cellular-interface contract itself, molecular-scale reaction-diffusion, sorting and phase separation, a self-maintained protocell, and colony development) are planned or in progress, not yet built; this file is honest about that.

What this investigation has NOT yet shown is the remaining patterns' spatial counterparts — reaction-diffusion molecular mechanisms, differential-adhesion sorting and phase separation, a self-maintained protocell, colony development, and the cellular-interface contract itself. Chemotaxis toward the field (the cell moving up the gradient it senses) is also deferred to a follow-up variant — the flagship cell's modest position drift over the run is CPM thermal fluctuation, not directed motion.

Introduction

This investigation is the spatial sibling of draft-to-living-cell. That investigation turned the cellular-interface paper's nine composition patterns into inert typed drafts compiled into small, lumped executables (ODE-scale mechanisms on 1-D or 9-cell grids). This investigation asks the harder question: do those same patterns still compose when the frameworks on each side of the interface are real, independently-built 2D spatial simulators — a Cellular Potts Model (viva-cpm, a Rust-backed lattice engine with genuine cell shape, adhesion, and volume dynamics) on one side, and spatio-flux's diffusion-advection PDE solver plus dynamic flux-balance analysis on the other? Composing two frameworks that were never designed with each other in mind is exactly the paper's central claim about typed interfaces made concrete: CpmCellField, the flagship's coupling process, is the interface where a CPM cell's footprint (a set of lattice pixels) is translated into a spatio-flux field read, and where a dynamic-FBA solution (mmol/gDW/hr fluxes) is translated back into a per-pixel field delta and a CPM target-volume update. Four studies are built and run so far — cell-environment-coupling-spatial (the flagship, the spatial analogue of Fig 5's sense/act loop), cell-cell-coupling-spatial, disintegration-spatial, and growth-and-division-spatial. The remaining patterns from draft-to-living-cell (molecular interfaces, biomolecular complementarity, autopoiesis, development-and-evolution, and the cellular-interface contract itself) are named as future increments in the design spec; some are planned, one (biomolecular complementarity) is in progress with code done but its study not yet accepted.

The investigations are deliberate analogues, study-for-study, wherever the counterpart exists: cell-environment-coupling-spatial here is the spatial realization of draft-to-living-cell/cell-environment-coupling. Read them side by side — the lumped 9-cell 1-D executable there against the real 60x60 2D CPM+spatio-flux composite here — for the same interface claim (sensing and acting are one coupling, not two subsystems; the cell measurably reshapes the gradient it depends on) demonstrated at two very different levels of mechanistic resolution.

Evidence for

  • cell-environment-coupling-spatial (the spatial Fig 5, this investigation's flagship) — a real CPM cell reads a spatio-flux glucose field at its own footprint, runs one dynamic-FBA step (e_coli_core, oxygen-capped to force acetate overflow), grows its CPM target volume from the biomass produced, and writes uptake/secretion back into the field as a spatial delta that DiffusionAdvection then spreads. Over 20 ticks: CPM volume grows ~3.4x (32 -> 110 px, ~3% of the 60x60 lattice — the cell does not fill it), local footprint glucose depletes up to ~18% before diffusion partially resupplies it, and the field-wide acetate total rises from 0 to ~47 (mmol-equivalent field units) as the secreted plume diffuses outward. Net field-wide glucose falls (~32 of 5940 total field mass consumed) even though the depletion signal is much sharper locally at the cell's own footprint than in the field-wide total — the direct niche-construction signature.

Evidence against

  • Five of the nine draft-to-living-cell patterns (molecular interfaces, biomolecular complementarity, autopoiesis, development-and-evolution, and the cellular-interface contract itself) have no spatial counterpart built and run yet — this investigation currently demonstrates four patterns, not nine.
  • Chemotaxis (directed up-gradient motion) is deferred; the flagship cell senses and acts on the field locally but does not move toward higher concentration.
  • The e_coli_core constants (glucose_km, glucose_vmax, oxygen_vmax, box_volume_L, grow_per_biomass) are toy-real — plausible, hand-tuned magnitudes chosen to produce gradual, spatially-legible dynamics over a short run, not fitted to any organism or medium.
  • The CPM lattice itself (the cell's shape, occupied pixels, footprint) lives inside viva-cpm's Rust engine as a private, write-protected world object (CpmCellField.world), not a process-bigraph store — only the coupling process reads and writes it. The "environment" the interface exposes to the rest of the composite is exclusively the spatio-flux fields store (glucose, acetate); the CPM's own internal field concept (field_at_cell, secretory_types) is separate machinery this composite does not use.

Findings

  • mechanism A real CPM cell coupled to a real spatio-flux nutrient field through one typed process (CpmCellField) closes the paper's §Cell–environment coupling sense/act loop (Fig 5) as genuine spatial metabolism: it runs dynamic-FBA at its own footprint and grows both biologically (biomass) and structurally (CPM lattice volume). Confirmed: field-wide acetate 0 -> ~47.0 over 20 ticks.

Cell–Cell Coupling, Spatial Accepted

Composing N real CPM cells with one shared, real spatio-flux nutrient field through a single N-cell coupling process (CpmColonyField) reproduces the paper's §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) as genuine spatial multicellularity, not a scripted or lumped stand-in for it: over 20-tick runs, an uptake-capacity asymmetry alone (glucose_vmax 10 vs 4) drives spatial competitive exclusion (biomass 237.9 vs 64.5, a 3.69x margin; volume 3511 vs 81 px), while a different composite over the same coupling process — a localized glucose depot, a fast-diffusing acetate byproduct, and an acetate-consuming role — sustains a cell that never touches glucose (local glucose 0.0) purely via a diffusing cross-feeding handoff (local acetate 1.30, biomass 1.25 -> 3.79). Both regimes run through the same process; only the composite's cell roles and field constants differ, mirroring draft-to-living-cell/cell-cell-coupling's law-4 pattern (two handler environments negotiate the shared-resource constraint differently over one interface) at real spatial, per-footprint metabolic resolution.

Question

Does the paper's §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) hold as real spatial multicellularity — multiple CPM cells sharing one diffusing nutrient field, each running its own dynamic-FBA step at its own footprint, producing both competitive exclusion and cross-feeding — when the whole colony is composed from independent frameworks through one N-cell coupling process (CpmColonyField), rather than the lumped two-role, shared-scalar-pool stand-in draft-to-living-cell's §Cell–cell coupling study used?

Findings

  • mechanism Multiple real CPM cells sharing one real spatio-flux nutrient field through one N-cell coupling process (CpmColonyField) reproduce the paper's §Cell–cell coupling competitive-exclusion branch (no dedicated figure) as genuine spatial multicellularity: an uptake-capacity asymmetry alone (glucose_vmax 10 vs 4), with each cell running its own independent dFBA solve at its own footprint on the same depleting field, drives a decisive biomass and lattice-volume split. Confirmed: biomass 237.9 vs 64.5 (3.69x), CPM volume 3511 vs 81 px, over a 20-tick run.
  • mechanism Over the same coupling process, a different composite -- a localized glucose depot, a fast-diffusing acetate byproduct, and an acetate-consuming cell role -- sustains a cell that never touches glucose purely via a spatial metabolic handoff: the consumer's local glucose reads 0.0 the entire run while its biomass still climbs, entirely on diffused acetate. This is the paper's §Cell–cell coupling relational-molecule claim made concrete -- the same acetate is the secretor's metabolic waste and the consumer's sole carbon food, its meaning set by each cell's interface (microbial cross-feeding, morris2013). Confirmed: consumer local_glucose 0.0, local_acetate 1.30, biomass 1.25 -> 3.79, ending as a viable 65-pixel CPM cell.
  • mechanism The additive two-writer assumption CpmColonyField's N-cell field update depends on -- that disjoint per-cell footprint deltas sum losslessly into one shared field -- is runtime-verified directly and independently of any dFBA mechanism, retiring what was previously an unverified assumption in the flagship study. Confirmed structurally: two synthetic single-pixel writers on disjoint pixels each apply independently and their combined effect exactly matches the sum of the two, over repeated ticks.

Disintegration, Spatial Accepted

Composing one real CPM cell with a real spatio-flux acetate field and a real spatio-flux particle-movement process through two coupling processes (CpmDisintegration for the viability-collapse trigger and resorption, stock BrownianMovement for the debris scatter) reproduces the paper's §Disintegration level-shift (Fig 6) as genuine spatial dissolution, not a scripted or lumped stand-in for it: over a deterministic 20-tick run, the cell holds coherent (area > 30) through tick 6, the footprint-local acetate mean crosses viability_threshold at released_tick = 7, the domain resorbs to area 0 by tick 16, and its 68 shed pixels become a debris cloud whose RMS spread strictly increases through tick 24 -- hold, cross, resorb, scatter, end to end from one coupling process plus one stock movement process, mirroring draft-to-living-cell/disintegration's §Disintegration level-shift pattern (Fig 6) at real spatial, per-pixel resolution.

Findings

  • mechanism A single real CPM cell coupled to a real spatio-flux acetate field through one coupling process (CpmDisintegration) reproduces the paper's §Disintegration level-shift (Fig 6) as genuine spatial dissolution: the cell holds coherent while the diffusing acetate field is below its footprint-local viability bound, then latches a release once that bound is crossed at a specific, deterministic tick, and its domain resorbs to full dissolution over the following ticks.   • mechanism The cell's shed material does not vanish -- it becomes physical debris. Confirmed: 68 particles shed by tick 16, RMS spread ~5.3 -> ~7.6 over an 8-tick post-dissolution window.
  • mechanism The CPM-to-particle bridge this study's disintegration mechanism depends on -- that a process can emit particles via the map _add sentinel into a shared map[particle] store, and a separate stock movement process can then move them -- is runtime-verified directly and independently of the disintegration mechanism itself, retiring what was previously an untested primitive. Confirmed structurally: a synthetic emitter's 3 particles remain exactly 3 particles four ticks later, with at least 2 having moved under BrownianMovement.

Growth and Division, Spatial Accepted

Composing one real CPM cell, growing via real per-footprint dFBA metabolism, with the native CPM engine operation divide_cells through a single coupling process (CpmGrowthDivision) reproduces the paper's §Growth and division growth-then-divide pattern (Fig 10a,b) as genuine spatial multicellular compounding, not a scripted lineage or a lumped stand-in for it: over a 36-tick run sampled every 3 ticks, the population steps up in a monotonic non-decreasing staircase (1,2,2,2,4,4,5,8,8,11,14,18), spanning roughly 3-4 generations, while every cell's CPM volume stays bounded in a sawtooth between reset_target = 40 and vol_threshold = 80 -- growth, crossing, division, and resumed growth in the daughters, all as direct simulation output from three independently-developed frameworks (viva-cpm + spatio-flux + cobra) meeting at one typed interface. This mirrors draft-to-living-cell/growth-and-division's §Growth and division claim (Fig 10a,b) (growth drives the cell's own state across a threshold; crossing the threshold triggers division) but realizes the division event itself as the native spatial CPM operation divide_cells acting directly on lattice pixels, in contrast to that study's place-graph rewrite (one node becoming two structurally-new nodes at a lumped, non-spatial level of composition).

Biomolecular Complementarity, Spatial Accepted

Differential-adhesion cell sorting reproduces the paper's §Molecular compositions complementarity (Fig 8) as a spatial mechanism: two cell types differing only in which contacts they find energetically favorable demix a well-mixed checkerboard (heterotypic-interface fraction ~0.64 -> ~0.06 over ~600 Monte Carlo steps) while staying cohesive (live-cell pixels ~1530, <1% drift; per-type counts constant at 32/32) -- like sitting with like is the spatial reading of which interface variables must align for coupling. Independently, a Cahn-Hilliard composition field realizes the condensate mechanism the paper draws from banani2017: a near-critical field phase-separates into two domains bounded by a sharp, emergent interface (variance ~7e-5 -> ~0.38 over 10000 steps) with mass conserved to ~4e-18 and the field bounded in [-1, 1]. The honest boundary of the claim is explicit: the condensate forms an interface but does not yet do interface work -- it does not selectively concentrate or exclude a second species -- so it demonstrates a boundary forming, not yet the functional, regulatable boundary the paper's central question distinguishes from a transient aggregate. Neither regime computes a binding affinity; both are tuned demonstration primitives, not fitted to a measured tissue or condensate. The two run as independent processes through the process-bigraph engine, with no coupling between them.

Question & findings

Question

The paper's §Molecular compositions section (Fig 8) frames its subject as a question about interfaces, not architecture: which patterns of molecular complementarity give rise to interfaces that behave as functional, regulatable boundaries, and which produce transient or unregulated aggregates? This study puts two spatial primitives of that question on the lattice. Differential-adhesion cell sorting -- Steinberg's Differential Adhesion Hypothesis -- is complementarity made spatial: a mixed population of two cell types, distinguished only by which contacts they find energetically favorable, demixes so that like sits with like and the heterotypic boundary between them collapses, a direct spatial reading of which interface variables must align for two cells to stay coupled. Biomolecular-condensate phase separation is the diffuse-interface counterpart: a scalar composition field, seeded near-critical, spontaneously separates into two phases divided by a sharp interface -- a boundary forming out of a well-mixed medium, the way a condensate droplet does. Each is a real 2D spatial mechanism run through the process-bigraph engine; the two are independent demonstrations, not one coupled model.

Findings

  • mechanism Differential-adhesion cell sorting reproduces the paper's §Molecular compositions complementarity (Fig 8) as a spatial mechanism: two cell types differing only in which contacts they find energetically favorable demix a well-mixed checkerboard so that like sits with like -- the spatial reading of which interface variables must align for coupling. Confirmed: heterotypic-interface fraction ~0.64 -> ~0.06 over ~600 MCS, while the clump stays cohesive (live-cell pixels ~1530, <1% drift) and per-type counts hold at 32/32.
  • mechanism A Cahn-Hilliard composition field realizes the condensate mechanism the paper draws from banani2017 (§Molecular compositions, Fig 8): weak-interaction-driven phase separation into droplets with an emergent interface. Confirmed as spinodal decomposition: a near-critical field separates into two phases (variance ~7e-5 -> ~0.38 over 10000 steps) bounded by a sharp interface, with total mass conserved to ~4e-18 and the field bounded in [-1, 1]. A boundary forms out of a well-mixed medium, mass-conserved.
  • observation Honesty point, load-bearing for the paper's central question. The condensate study demonstrates a boundary forming -- phase separation, mass-conserved, with an emergent interface -- but NOT the condensate doing interface work: it does not selectively concentrate or exclude a second species. That selective-permeability property is exactly what would make the interface a functional, regulatable boundary rather than a transient or unregulated aggregate, which is the discriminator the paper's §Molecular compositions question (Fig 8) turns on. It is named here as the natural next step, not claimed as already shown. Likewise the sorting study demonstrates differential-adhesion demixing with a cohesion guard, not a molecular binding-affinity/selectivity term.
  • mechanism The demixing is caused by the differential-J regime, not by CPM dynamics per se. Under neutral contact energies (all J=8) the same checkerboard at T=10 stays mixed, isolating the complementarity-encoding contact-energy asymmetry as the cause of sorting. Confirmed by a neutral-J composite re-run (documented, not a committed test): neutral J holds hetero_frac ~0.52 over 600 MCS vs ~0.06 for the differential-J regime.

Method & shared caveats

Every figure begins as an inert draft — typed, unit-bearing ports and a behavior contract, no dynamics — and is compiled by installing one conforming mechanism, which preserves the interface exactly (compiler law 2) while giving the figure real dynamics. Readouts below are measured, not asserted: every executable and the whole cell were run and their observables recorded.

Limitations (shared across every study)

  • This investigation is still mid-buildout: four of nine planned studies are built and run (the flagship plus cell-cell-coupling-spatial, disintegration-spatial, and growth-and-division-spatial). The design spec (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-21-cellular-interface-multicellular-design.md) lays out a complete 9-study analogy to draft-to-living-cell; this file reflects the current increment, not the finished plan.
  • Every mechanism here is toy-real: plausible constants, not calibrated to a named organism, medium, or literature target. The dynamics demonstrate the coupling pattern (real spatial sensing, metabolism, growth, secretion, diffusion), not a quantitatively validated cell.
  • Read alongside its specification-stage analogue, draft-to-living-cell/cell-environment-coupling: that study closes the same Fig 5 loop over a lumped 9-cell 1-D grid with a scripted-shape cell; this study closes it over a real 60x60 2D CPM cell with genuine shape and a real dynamic-FBA metabolism. Compare the two for the same interface claim at two very different mechanistic resolutions.
