## A. Paper mining — the richest unused material, per pattern

The studies quote the paper's figure numbers but almost none of its *language, mechanisms, or distinctions*. The paper is written; the studies should be spending its vocabulary. Pattern by pattern:

### A1. Cellular interface (Fig 4; §"Cellular interface") → flagship + the planned `cellular-interface` contract study

- **The four physical ports, with units.** "At minimum, the cellular interface consists of physical ports mediating exchange of matter and energy, including chemical fluxes (mol·s⁻¹), mechanical forces (N), electrical currents (C·s⁻¹), and heat transfer (J·s⁻¹)." All four studies realize **only the chemical port**. The bitter irony: the CPM is natively a *mechanical* model (adhesion energies, volume constraint, contact J, surface tension), and the design spec (§"Force / mechanical interface") already planned viva-cpm force/tension/pressure ports — none surfaced. Every study runs a mechanical engine and reports zero mechanical interface variables.
- **Conservation laws as interface constraints.** "They are constrained by conservation laws such as mass and energy balance, which coupled internal processes to external exchange" (cites beard2007, heinrich2012). The flagship's own Next-steps bullet ("track field-wide mass … as an explicit closed balance") is the paper's demand — still unmet in studies 2–4. Energy balance appears nowhere.
- **Substitutability — the paper's central thesis, undemonstrated.** §2: "This substitutability is not only a software convenience. It allows the interface itself to become the object of comparison: different mechanisms may realize the same externally observable relation." And §"Cellular interface": "different internal organizations may sometimes be represented through the same coarse-grained interface when they preserve the interaction variables relevant to that description." **No spatial study ever swaps a mechanism behind a held-fixed interface.** This is the single most paper-faithful experiment available and it is missing (see E-1).
- **Viability bounds, graded.** "At first these bounds may be supplied phenomenologically — for example, ranges of temperature, pH, osmolarity, or nutrient availability — but a more mechanistic model should allow them to emerge from the failure of the processes that sustain the cellular interface." The disintegration study's `viability_threshold` is exactly the "at first" case and never says so (see A4, B-3).
- **Minimal agency vocabulary.** "not as a representation or conscious intention, but as an endogenous tendency to act in ways that preserve the conditions of its own continued existence." Free upgrade for the flagship's framing — currently the biological_summary is all plumbing ("typed port", "map[array]") and no agency.
- **CBO** (sluka2014): a structured vocabulary for interface variables (adhesion, migration, proliferation, signaling) — a ready-made naming scheme for the observables the studies invent ad hoc (`local_nutrient`, `acetate_secreted`, …).

### A2. Cell–environment coupling (Fig 5) → `cell-environment-coupling-spatial`

- **"This feedback loop is the minimal structure required for control."** The flagship claims a sense/act loop but the cell exercises no control — it only takes up what's there. Quote the line and be explicit that the loop is closed (depletion feeds back into next-tick uptake) while *control* (chemotaxis, regulation) is the deferred half. The spec (§"The flagship") promised "chemotaxes up-gradient" and a "moves up-gradient" behavior test; both were silently dropped between spec and study (see D-9).
- **Niche construction has a citation waiting.** "Cells thereby reshape their environment as they respond to it, often constructing the very gradients and conditions they depend on \cite{odling1996niche}." The study uses the phrase "niche construction" three times and cites nothing.
- **Coupling strength is set by transport.** "Processes such as diffusion, advection, and mechanical relaxation set how quickly perturbations propagate and therefore how strongly cells are coupled through space." This is a directly sweepable claim (vary D, measure depletion-halo radius / cross-feeding lag) that no study tests — the crossfeed study *tunes* D but never *varies* it as the experiment.
- **The policy-vs-mechanism passage** ("agent-based models … appropriate when internal mechanisms can be abstracted into policies/rules. When the question shifts to how those policies arise or fail, the internal processes generating interface behavior must be made explicit") is the exact philosophical contrast between this investigation and `draft-to-living-cell`, stated better than any study currently states it. Quote it in the investigation lead.
- **"The environment … becomes both a medium of interaction and a distributed state through which multiple interfaces are indirectly coupled"** — the one-sentence bridge from the flagship to the colony study; currently the two studies are stitched with engineering prose instead.

### A3. Cell–cell coupling (no figure; §"Cell–cell coupling") → `cell-cell-coupling-spatial`

- **The headline miss — the paper's closing claim, embodied and unclaimed.** Discussion, final paragraph: "environmental variables acquire significance relationally, a molecule counting as nutrient, signal, or toxin through its consequences for viability." The cross-feeding regime is a *perfect demonstration*: acetate is overflow waste to the secretor and sole carbon source to the consumer — the same molecule, two relational meanings, decided by each cell's interface. The study never says this. It is the best sentence available to this whole investigation and it's on the floor.
- **"What is coupled is not only state, but constraint."** The study couples state (the shared field) but never constraint: no cell has a viability bound, so "viability negotiation" (the study's own title-phrase) is asserted, not measured. The paper continues: cells "can push others outside their viable region through nutrient depletion, toxin-mediated interference, or mechanical competition that promotes cell elimination \cite{ghoul2016ecology,levayer2016tissue}, or stabilize them through metabolic cooperation \cite{morris2013microbial}." Three concrete offensive mechanisms; the study realizes only nutrient depletion, and even there the "loser" ends at biomass 64.5 and 81 px — displaced, never pushed out of any viable region (see B-2).
- **The direct-interaction inventory**: cadherin/integrin adhesion, Notch–Delta signaling, gap junctions (electrical!), paracrine exchange. All coupling in the study is field-mediated; *contact* coupling — CPM's home turf, differential adhesion, mechanical competition — is untouched. A mechanical-competition regime (two cells crowding, elimination by pressure — levayer2016's actual subject) would use the engine for what it's for.
- **morris2013 is literally a cross-feeding citation** sitting in the paper, uncited by the cross-feeding study.
- **"Multicellular organization then appears as the ongoing negotiation of viability across a population"** — the sentence the study's `claim` paraphrases badly; quote the original.

### A4. Disintegration (Fig 6) → `disintegration-spatial`

- **The failure cascade.** "As these bounds are crossed, regulatory processes fail: energy production can no longer sustain transport, membranes lose integrity, gradients collapse, and feedback mechanisms break down. The system transitions from an actively maintained, far-from-equilibrium state to one dominated by diffusion, equilibration, and mechanical relaxation." The study's post-collapse regime — BrownianMovement debris — *is* "dominated by diffusion"; the narrative never draws the far-from-equilibrium → equilibrium reading, which would elevate the debris from a rendering trick to the paper's thermodynamic point.
- **Direct ammunition, unused**: "Most models do not represent this transition explicitly: agent-based models remove cells mid-simulation, and whole-cell models assume viability." This is the sentence that justifies the study's existence. Put it in the biological_summary.
- **Interface-semantics shift**: "The interface ceases to function as a regulated boundary and becomes a passive region of exchange." In the study nothing was ever exchanged across the boundary (no metabolism), so the semantics can't shift — the boundary goes from geometric to gone. See B-3.
- **The reverse move** (Fig 6b caption): "a molecular reaction network can be replaced by a coarse-grained metabolism process when closed loops of catalyzed reactions are identified" — coarse-graining back up is the unbuilt second half of the level-shift and a natural follow-up study.
- **An emergent viability bound already exists in the codebase, unused.** `cell_field.py::_fba` documents that at zero footprint glucose the LP goes *infeasible because the fixed ATP maintenance demand cannot be met* — that is viability failure emerging from process failure, precisely the paper's "more mechanistic model should allow them to emerge from the failure of the processes that sustain the interface." The disintegration study deliberately stripped out metabolism and installed a phenomenological threshold instead. The mechanistic trigger was one import away.
- **Named stressors**: nutrient depletion, toxin accumulation, osmotic imbalance, mechanical stress, thermal extremes. The study's stressor is anonymous. Choosing *toxin accumulation* and making the toxin **acetate** would (a) name the stressor, (b) reuse the investigation's own byproduct chemistry, and (c) close the relational loop of A3: one molecule as waste, nutrient, and toxin across three studies — the paper's final line as a three-study arc.
- Cites available: mcshaffrey2025matters, beer2024deriving (for the level-shift-as-representation-change claim), nicholls2013bioenergetics (for the maintained gradients).

### A5. Molecular interfaces (Fig 7; planned) 

For the in-progress work: the paper's four molecular channels (chemical, electrostatic, mechanical, thermal), molecules as "spatial, dynamic entities with position, orientation, and conformation," and the framing of PDB/Reactome/ChEBI/GO as "partial specifications of molecular interfaces and their types" — the last is a genuinely original claim worth one study hook (map a Reactome pathway fragment onto typed process ports).

### A6. Biomolecular complementarity (Fig 8; in progress: sorting + Cahn–Hilliard)

- The paper's condensate paragraph is the design brief: weak multivalent interactions → phase separation → droplets with **emergent surface tension, viscosity, and selective permeability** (banani2017); new interface variables: **binding states, complex composition, local density, phase boundaries**.
- **The question to test is stated verbatim**: "The central question becomes which patterns of complementarity give rise to interfaces that behave as functional, regulatable boundaries, and which produce transient or unregulated aggregates." A CH study that only shows spinodal decomposition demonstrates none of this. Make the condensate *do interface work*: show a droplet selectively concentrating or excluding a second species ("condensates … can act as partial compartments, selectively concentrating or excluding molecules without a fixed membrane") — that's the pass/fail worth having.
- Cell sorting = "complementarity made spatial" per the spec; tie it to the paper's "which interface variables must align for coupling to occur" rather than just Steinberg.

### A7. Autopoiesis (Fig 9; planned)

The pass/fail criterion is in the text: "A membrane alone is insufficient: a vesicle may form a boundary without constituting a living system. A stronger criterion is that the processes inside the boundary collectively contribute to maintaining the organization that, in turn, keeps those processes possible." So the study needs a *negative control vesicle* (boundary without closure, decays) against a closed metabolism-membrane-repair loop (persists) — the contrast is the finding. Also: "viability bounds arise as constraints on the shared state that must be maintained for the composition to persist" (beer2024deriving) — this is where the phenomenological threshold of A4 finally becomes emergent.

### A8. Growth, division, development, evolution (Fig 10) → `growth-and-division-spatial` + planned dev/evo

- **Division partitions state; the study resets it.** Paper: "Division is a compositional rewrite … with state partitioned across daughter systems … Each daughter inherits a subset of stores and processes." Fig 10b caption: "partitions state variables such as DNA and biomass into two cells." In `growth_division.py::update`, both daughters' biomass is **reset to `reset_target/grow_per_biomass`**, discarding the parent's actual biomass (any overshoot past threshold evaporates). Pixels are conserved; biological state is re-initialized, not inherited. The study's "mass-conserved" language covers only lattice pixels and the limitation never names the biomass reset. This is both a fidelity bug against Fig 10b and an easy fix (split tracked biomass in half at division).
- **"Coordination and divergence" — a real finding treated as noise.** Paper: "Immediately after division, these systems remain coupled through shared environmental state, so their interfaces are not independent … enabling both coordination and divergence." The measured staircase `…8,8,8,13,16` shows division *desynchronizing* in generation 4 — precisely divergence induced by shared-field depletion and crowding. The study buries this in a trajectory string; it should be a finding (F-04: lockstep breaks when local glucose heterogeneity makes sibling growth rates diverge), with a per-cell growth-rate-vs-position readout.
- **Persisters**: "the interface is reconfigured to maintain viability under constrained conditions, as seen in bacterial persisters" — the natural growth-arrest variant (deplete the field, show growth downshift rather than death).
- **Biofilm hierarchy** for dev/evo: "individual cellular interfaces become embedded within a collective interface defined by shared state and constraints" (stewart2008 — physiological heterogeneity; flemming2016). The 16-cell colony already has a depleted core and fed rim; measuring center-vs-edge growth is the first step toward "collective interface."
- **Facilitated variation** (kirschner2005): "interfaces and wiring are themselves evolvable features of organization, not fixed contracts" — the design principle for the planned evolution study (mutate *ports/couplings*, not just parameters).
- **The adaptation ladder** — regulation / learning / evolution distinguished purely by whether "past interactions alter future coupling" — is the cleanest possible organizing rubric for studies 2 → 9 and appears nowhere in the investigation narrative.

## B. Scientific shortcomings

### B-0. Cross-cutting: the composition is thinner than the claims say

1. **The bigraph is degenerate — the paper's architecture is not actually realized per cell.** Fig 1 and §2 put *each cell* behind its own interface ports, cells nested in hierarchies, rewrites mutating the composition ("Rewrites thus make the composition itself mutable"). In all four studies the entire cellular world lives inside **one monolithic coupling process** (`CpmCellField`/`CpmColonyField`/`CpmGrowthDivision` "owns" the world; study.yamls admit the lattice "is not a process-bigraph store"). Cells are not processes; they have no ports; division mints an id inside a Rust object and **the composition graph never changes** — the exact opposite of the paper's rewrite claim the growth study cites. The studies' honest paragraphs treat this as an engine footnote; it is a *substantive divergence from the thesis being demonstrated* and should be confronted head-on in investigation.yaml (what is demonstrated: framework-level composition through one typed interface; what is not: per-cell interfaces, hierarchical nesting, structural rewrites).
2. **One modality out of four.** Chemical only. No mechanical interface variables despite a mechanical engine and a spec commitment (see A1); no electrical, no thermal, and no study even names the absence using the paper's modality vocabulary.
3. **No units, no timescale.** "Tick" is never mapped to time; FBA constants are in mmol/gDW/hr but `box_volume_L` differs by study (0.3 in the flagship study.yaml vs 1e-6 code default), lattice pixels have no physical size, diffusion coefficients are unitless. Toy-real is fine; *dimensionally incoherent* toy-real invites the first skeptical question every time.
4. **Single seed, no replicates.** CPM is stochastic (temperature 10–11, `seed: 1` hard-coded in every process). All headline numbers (3.69x, 110 px, released_tick == 7) are one-seed values; "deterministic across repeated runs" means "same seed, same answer." No seed sweep, no bands, no sensitivity analysis anywhere. The `viva-harden-investigation` bar would flag every study.
5. **"Toy-real, not fitted" is drifting from honesty toward exemption.** Cheap, real anchors exist and are ignored: (a) e_coli_core's acetate/glucose flux ratio under O2 cap can be checked against measured overflow stoichiometry (~0.3–0.7 mol/mol above the critical growth rate — Basan et al. 2015-type data); (b) the crossfeed D ratio of 15.0/0.4 = **37.5x** vs the real aqueous acetate/glucose ratio of roughly **~2x** (≈1.2e-5 vs 6.7e-6 cm²/s) — "directionally defensible" is true and quantitatively off by ~20x; say the number. Dimensionless anchors cost nothing and would convert several hand-waves into calibration statements.
6. **Figure-citation error.** "Fig 3 viability negotiation" (spec, study question/claim/findings) does not exist in the current manuscript; Fig 3 is orchestration, and cell–cell coupling has no figure. Presumably inherited from a v1 numbering. Every downstream artifact repeats it.
7. **Stale scope prose.** The flagship's limitations still say "This is the investigation's only built study so far — the remaining eight … do not exist yet," and investigation.yaml's hypothesis/lead say "only the flagship … is built and run so far" — contradicted by the other three Accepted studies in the same tree.

### B-1. `cell-environment-coupling-spatial`

- **The mechanism claim about the O2 cap is argued from code, not from a run.** F-03 asserts "the O2 cap … forces the optimizer into acetate overflow." The direct evidence is a control the study doesn't run: same composite, `oxygen_vmax` unbounded → acetate ≈ 0. One variant, one row in the outcomes table, and the mechanism claim becomes an experiment. (Studies 2–4 each carry a foundational control; the flagship — built first — has none. Backport the convention.)
- **"Sense/act" without act.** The only "action" is uptake/secretion, which is passive consequence of metabolism; the paper's loop is "the minimal structure required for control," and the promised control behavior (chemotaxis; the spec's "moves up-gradient" test) was dropped. The claim language should downgrade to "sense/metabolize/secrete loop" or the chemotaxis variant should be built.
- **Mass balance still open** (study's own next step) — with secretion and uptake both flowing through hand-written per-pixel deltas, a closed source/sink ledger is the one control that would catch a mass leak, and it's the paper's own conservation-law demand.
- Behavior-test thresholds are near-tautological (biomass > 0.1 when observed 0.37; acetate > 0.0): gates that cannot fail except on crash. Fine as regression floors; not "biological validation: PASS" material without at least a band.

### B-2. `cell-cell-coupling-spatial`

- **"Competitive exclusion" overclaims.** The loser retains 64.5 biomass units and 81 px at t=20; nothing excludes it — no death, no viability bound, no starvation endpoint. What is demonstrated is *asymmetric growth under shared-resource competition*. Worse, the winner ends at **3511/3600 px ≈ 97.5% of the lattice** — the endpoint is lattice saturation, i.e. the margin (3.69x biomass, 43x volume) is dominated by finite-size artifacts, and the glucose field is nearly exhausted (t=20 frame is uniformly dark), so both cells are entering starvation just as the run stops. A skeptic reads this as "you stopped the run at the flattering frame."
- **No viability anywhere in the "viability negotiation" study.** Not one variable, bound, or test mentions viability. Add a minimal viable-biomass floor or starvation criterion, and the exclusion branch gets teeth (does the loser actually get pushed below it?) — this is the paper's "state vs constraint" distinction (A3).
- **Cross-feeding lacks its necessity control**: consumer with the secretor deleted (or O2 cap removed so no overflow) should fail to grow. Currently the handoff is supported by local_glucose == 0.0 + growth, which is strong, but the one-composite control closes it.
- **Contact coupling untested even where it happens.** In the compete run the two cells visibly collide (t=12 frame); cell–cell contact energies exist in the config but no observable measures shared boundary length, and the study's story stays purely field-mediated. The paper's direct-interaction channel is silently present and unmeasured.
- The `crossfeeding-acetate-plume-reaches-consumer` diagnostic and `two-writer-additivity` are labeled `control: negative` — they are positive/structural controls. Mislabels like this erode trust in the schema fields.

### B-3. `disintegration-spatial`

- **Phenomenological bound presented without the paper's own grading.** The paper explicitly ranks threshold-supplied viability as the starting point and emergent-from-process-failure as the goal (A1/A4). The study should cite that sentence, and the roadmap should name the mechanistic version — which, per A4, already exists as FBA-maintenance infeasibility in `cell_field.py`. Stripping metabolism out of the one study that is *about* metabolic collapse ("energy production can no longer sustain transport") is the pattern realized with its causal core removed: the trigger is exogenous weather, not endogenous failure.
- **Debris has no chemical identity.** Shed pixels become inert markers no other process reads. The paper's post-collapse world is "interacting molecular components" — at minimum the debris should deposit mass back into a field (lysate as nutrient for a neighbor — again the relational-molecule arc, and it would make disintegration composable with the colony studies rather than a dead end).
- **Mass accounting between pixels and particles is not asserted.** Area at release is 56, shed particles total 68 (8/tick cap). Presumably resorption plus fluctuation vacates more pixels than the release-time area, but no test asserts shed_particles == vacated_pixels, so "shed material, not deleted mass" is only mostly checked.
- The claimed determinism is fixed-seed determinism for the CPM but `BrownianMovement` is unseeded (the study admits RMS magnitudes vary run to run) — the study is half-deterministic and the framing should stop leaning on the word.

### B-4. `growth-and-division-spatial`

- **Division resets state instead of partitioning it** (see A8; `growth_division.py` lines ~240–251). Fig 10b's caption says division "partitions state variables such as DNA and biomass into two cells"; here both daughters get `reset_target/grow_per_biomass` regardless of the parent's actual biomass, so biological mass is destroyed at every division event while lattice pixels are conserved. The claim "mass-conserved" is technically scoped to pixels but reads as more. Fix (split biomass proportional to daughter volumes) is ~5 lines and makes the `native-division-conserves-mass` control meaningful at both levels.
- **"Lineage" without a lineage.** No genealogy is recorded (which parent minted which daughter id is available from `divide_cells`' return and discarded). The 1→2→4→8→16 narrative is a population count, not a lineage tree; per Fig 10a,b language ("daughter systems … inherit") the tree is the object.
- **The 13-in-the-staircase is the most interesting untold result** (division desynchronization via shared-field coupling — the paper's "coordination and divergence," A8). Currently the study's framing treats non-powers-of-two as a nuisance survived rather than the first emergent multicellular heterogeneity in the investigation.
- No division-related behavior test touches the *field*: with `glucose_vmax = 1.5` on an abundant uniform field, glucose barely dips (visible in the GIF — see C-4), so "bounded by the shared glucose field" in the claim is asserted; the run never demonstrates nutrient limitation shaping the staircase (a longer run or a leaner field would).

## C. Aesthetic & narrative shortcomings

Judged from extracted frames (first/mid/last) of all five GIFs plus `viz.py` and the metrics HTML code.

### C-1. `cellcell-compete.gif` — the worst legibility failure in the set

The final frame reads as **an empty dark field with two stray circles**. The winner has engulfed ~97% of the lattice, so its red *contour* coincides with the frame border and disappears; the loser is confetti in a corner; the glucose field is uniformly depleted dark purple. The story is "winner takes all" and the image says "everyone died." Fixes, in order of value:
- Fill footprints with translucent per-id color over the field (contours only work when cells are small islands);
- Add a glucose **depletion (Δ from t=0)** panel like the flagship's — the raw field saturates dark and goes visually dead by mid-run;
- 10 frames for a 20-tick takeover is choppy; capture cadence 1;
- There is **no metrics panel at all for the competition regime** (only crossfeed has one) — the 3.69x margin, the study's headline number, is never plotted anywhere.

### C-2. `growth-division-spatial.gif` — contour-on-yellow contrast failure

The uniform abundant field renders as saturated yellow (variation ~8–12 plotted on a 0–12 scale), and `_ID_PALETTE`'s gold/sandybrown/orchid contours are **nearly invisible on it** (the t=36 frame's outer cells vanish). Colors recycle after 6 ids, so among 16 cells distinct cells share colors, and colors encode nothing (siblings unrelated). Fixes: tighten the color scale to the field's actual range or show Δ-from-t0 so the colony's depletion bowl — the actual biology — becomes visible; fill footprints; color by **lineage** (founder hue inherited, shade stepped per generation) so the movie shows a genealogy, not a crowd; annotate division events (flash the splitting cell's outline, or a frame-title counter "division #k at t=…").

### C-3. `single-cell-in-a-field.gif` — competent, sterile

The three-panel design (raw field / Δ-depletion / acetate) is the right idea and the Δ panel is genuinely good. Problems: the white footprint contour is invisible on the white midfield of the diverging panel in early frames; the COM marker changes color per panel (crimson/black/deepskyblue) for no reason — pick one accent; the cell reads as an empty outline rather than a body (light fill would fix); the suptitle is a repo slug ("single-cell-in-a-field: CPM cell over its nutrient field") repeated identically on every frame — retitle with the pattern ("Cell–environment coupling: the sense/act loop, Fig 5") and put `t` in one shared place. Nothing in the frame narrates the loop itself — two small annotated arrows (glc → cell, cell → ac) would earn the "sense/act" name visually.

### C-4. `cellcell-crossfeed.gif` — good story, cluttered climax

The two-panel glucose/acetate layout works and the mid-run frame (plume crossing toward the consumer) is the best storytelling frame in the investigation. But by t≥12 the secretor's contour shatters into ragged fragments that read as noise; the legend overlaps the field; the GIF's 256-color quantization visibly bands the magma colorbar; the final glucose panel is again a flat dead purple. And the run's dramatic moment — plume arrival, the tick the consumer's growth turns on — is unmarked in both GIF and metrics. Add an "acetate reaches consumer (t≈15)" marker; the disintegration panel already proves the team knows how (`add_vline` at released_tick — the only event marker in the whole set; generalize it).

### C-5. `disintegration-spatial.gif` — the keeper, one upgrade short of excellent

Inferno radial stressor + cyan cell + white debris is legible and even handsome; the filled-footprint choice (documented in `viz.py`) is exactly right. Two upgrades: (1) draw the **viability isoline** — the `stressor == viability_threshold` contour — as a moving front; the audience would literally watch the bound close in on the cell and touch it at t=7, which is the paper's Fig 6 in one visual device; (2) mark release in-frame (title or border color shift at released_tick) — currently the movie's pivotal event is only findable in the separate metrics HTML. Optional: debris age-fade or short trails so scatter reads as motion in still frames.

### C-6. Metrics panels & report narrative

- The four panels share no visual identity beyond `plotly_white` (different titles styles, key-dependent layouts, `_PALETTE` for metrics vs `_ID_PALETTE` for cells with no mapping between GIF cell colors and metrics trace colors except in the colony case). A reader moving between GIF and panel should be able to carry colors across — only `_metrics_panel_percell` does this.
- Event markers: only disintegration has one. Division ticks, plume arrival, threshold crossings all belong on the time axes.
- **Narrative voice is engineering-first.** Every study's question/claim leads with "two independently-built simulators … one typed coupling process," verbatim-similar across all four, and the biology arrives in subordinate clauses. The paper's voice is the reverse. One pass swapping the paper's sentences (Part A) into `question`/`biological_summary` — leading with the negotiation, the collapse, the compounding lineage, and keeping the composition brag to one sentence — would transform how the report reads. The word "toy-real" appears ~20 times across four studies; keep the honesty, vary the prose.
- The repeated per-study litany "this is the Nth of nine planned studies; M remain unbuilt (list)" is investigation-level scope stapled into every study, already stale in one of them (B-0.7). State it once, in investigation.yaml, and lint it.

## D. Process gaps — what the pipeline systematically misses

1. **No staleness discipline for cross-study references.** Studies assert investigation-level facts ("only built study," "seven remain unbuilt") that rot as siblings land. Rule: studies never count siblings; investigation.yaml owns scope; lint for phrases like "only built study."
2. **No provenance check of paper citations.** Figure numbers were inherited from an older numbering (Fig 3) and propagated through spec → study → findings → captions unverified. Rule: cite the paper by **section title + quoted phrase** (stable) rather than figure number alone; re-verify against the current main.tex at study-accept time.
3. **Zero literature citations in study.yamls** despite the source paper carrying a curated bibliography for every pattern (odling1996, morris2013, levayer2016, banani2017, stewart2008, kirschner2005 …). The pipeline has a cite mechanism (`viva-cite-bands`) that goes unused because no bands exist — which points at:
4. **Acceptance criteria are crash-floors, not bands.** `> 0.1` when observed 0.37, `> 0.0` booleans. The pipeline should require, for at least one primary test per study, either a literature-anchored band (even dimensionless — overflow ratio, D-ratio, division sawtooth ratio ≈ 2) or an explicit statement of why none is possible.
5. **No robustness stage.** Single hard-coded seed everywhere; no multi-seed replicate, no parameter perturbation, before `confidence: Accepted`. Cheap version: 5 seeds, report range next to the headline number.
6. **Controls convention evolved forward but never backported**, and control semantics are mislabeled (`control: negative` on positive/structural verifications). Define control taxonomy once; audit the flagship (which has none).
7. **No shared constants ledger.** `grow_per_biomass` 300 vs 30, `oxygen_vmax` 2.5 vs 5 vs 15, `glucose_vmax` 3.5/10/4/1.5 across studies, with per-study prose justifications but no single table of regimes and rationales. Cross-study coherence is currently maintained in the reviewer's head.
8. **Viz is bespoke-per-study with a fragile dispatch.** Four hand-rolled renderers in one 950-line `viz.py`; `metrics_panel` routes on key-sniffing (`if "n_cells" in metrics …`) that will misroute the fifth study; styling decisions (palettes, titles, markers, event lines) re-made each time. Extract a tiny frame-style contract (field colormap policy incl. Δ panels, footprint fill + contour, one accent for COM, event-marker helper, one title scheme) before the next five studies multiply the debt.
