Q The paper's §Molecular compositions section (Fig 7) frames its subject as a question about interfaces, not architecture: which patterns of molecular complementarity give rise to interfaces that behave as functional, regulatable boundaries, and which produce transient or unregulated aggregates? This study puts two spatial primitives of that question on the lattice. Differential-adhesion cell sorting -- Steinberg's Differential Adhesion Hypothesis -- is complementarity made spatial: a mixed population of two cell types, distinguished only by which contacts they find energetically favorable, demixes so that like sits with like and the heterotypic boundary between them collapses, a direct spatial reading of which interface variables must align for two cells to stay coupled. Biomolecular-condensate phase separation is the diffuse-interface counterpart: a scalar composition field, seeded near-critical, spontaneously separates into two phases divided by a sharp interface -- a boundary forming out of a well-mixed medium, the way a condensate droplet does. Each is a real 2D spatial mechanism run through the process-bigraph engine; the two are independent demonstrations, not one coupled model.

Claim

Differential-adhesion cell sorting reproduces the paper's §Molecular compositions complementarity (Fig 7) as a spatial mechanism: two cell types differing only in which contacts they find energetically favorable demix a well-mixed checkerboard (heterotypic-interface fraction ~0.64 -> ~0.06 over ~600 Monte Carlo steps) while staying cohesive (live-cell pixels ~1530, <1% drift; per-type counts constant at 32/32) -- like sitting with like is the spatial reading of which interface variables must align for coupling. Independently, a Cahn-Hilliard composition field realizes the condensate mechanism the paper draws from banani2017: a near-critical field phase-separates into two domains bounded by a sharp, emergent interface (variance ~7e-5 -> ~0.38 over 10000 steps) with mass conserved to ~4e-18 and the field bounded in [-1, 1]. The honest boundary of the claim is explicit: the condensate forms an interface but does not yet do interface work -- it does not selectively concentrate or exclude a second species -- so it demonstrates a boundary forming, not yet the functional, regulatable boundary the paper's central question distinguishes from a transient aggregate. Neither regime computes a binding affinity; both are tuned demonstration primitives, not fitted to a measured tissue or condensate. The two run as independent processes through the process-bigraph engine, with no coupling between them.

Steinberg's differential-adhesion sorting is the paper's §Molecular compositions complementarity (Fig 7) read spatially: molecules bind the partners they recognize and not others, and at the scale of cells that same selectivity shows up as which contacts are cheap and which are costly. Give two cell types a contact-energy matrix in which homotypic contact is favorable and heterotypic contact is expensive, and a well-mixed checkerboard self-organizes -- like cells cluster, unlike cells pull apart, and the heterotypic interface shrinks. That is the question of which interface variables must align for coupling to occur, made mechanical: cells stay adjacent exactly where their adhesion preferences are complementary. The condensate side realizes the paper's other §Molecular compositions mechanism (Fig 7). Following banani2017 as the paper does, weak multivalent interactions among biomolecules can drive liquid-liquid phase separation into droplets that acquire emergent physical properties -- surface tension, viscosity, and selective permeability -- and that introduce new interface variables the surrounding system must now reckon with: binding states, complex composition, local density, and phase boundaries. Here that is modeled as a Cahn-Hilliard scalar composition field: a double-well free energy plus an interfacial-gradient penalty drives mass-conserving spinodal decomposition, so a near-critical field separates into two phases bounded by a sharp, emergent interface. Measured this build. SORTING (meta_modelers_guide.composites.cell-sorting-spatial): an 8x8 checkerboard of 64 cells (32 type-1 / 32 type-2, 5x5 px each, 70x70 lattice) demixes over ~600 Monte Carlo steps -- the heterotypic-interface fraction collapses from a clearly-mixed ~0.64 at the first observation (the raw seeded checkerboard is ~1.0 before any relaxation) to ~0.06, roughly an order-of-magnitude drop, while a cohesion guard confirms the clump did not merely dissolve: total live-cell pixels hold near ~1530 (well under the 10% drift bound) and the per-type counts stay exactly 32/32. The cohesion guard is load-bearing, not decoration -- the heterotypic-interface fraction alone falls to 0.0 when the cells boil off into medium at high temperature, which would read as a perfect sort of an empty lattice, so a dissolved clump must never be misread as sorted. CONDENSATE (meta_modelers_guide.composites.condensate-cahn-hilliard): a 64x64 composition field seeded with near-critical noise (mean 0, +/-0.025) phase-separates over 10000 steps -- the field variance rises from ~7e-5 to ~0.38 as two domains form, total mass is conserved to ~4e-18 (the composition is redistributed, not created or destroyed), the field stays bounded in [-1, 1], and no NaN appears. Honesty point. The condensate study demonstrates a boundary forming -- phase separation, mass-conserved, with an emergent interface -- but it does not yet demonstrate the condensate doing interface work: selectively concentrating or excluding a second species, the property that would let it act as a functional, regulatable compartment rather than a transient aggregate. That is precisely the discriminator the paper's §Molecular compositions question turns on, and it is the natural next step, not something this study already shows. The sorting study likewise demonstrates differential-adhesion demixing with a cohesion guard, not molecular binding-affinity selectivity: there is no partner-choice term, exactly as a coarser, specification-stage lumped realization scopes its own selectivity gap. Both regimes are tuned, not fitted: the sorting J-triple (homotypic 2, heterotypic 11, medium 8) and temperature 10 sit in a narrow cohesive-and-mobile window (T=1 freezes the mixed state, T>=200 dissolves the cells), and the Cahn-Hilliard scheme is numerically stiff, its timestep pinned at dt=0.002 against the biharmonic stability limit. This is the spatial counterpart to the lumped ODE predecessor, which built the same complementarity pattern as a nested-hierarchy expression cascade; read the two side by side for the same interface idea at two very different mechanistic resolutions.

Conclusion

Claims

  • Differential-adhesion cell sorting reproduces the paper's §Molecular compositions complementarity (Fig 7) as a spatial mechanism: two cell types differing only in which contacts they find favorable demix a well-mixed checkerboard (hetero_frac ~0.64 -> ~0.06 over ~600 MCS) while staying cohesive (pixels ~1530, <1% drift; counts 32/32). A neutral-J control (all J=8) does not sort, isolating the contact-energy asymmetry as the cause.

  • A Cahn-Hilliard composition field realizes the condensate mechanism the paper draws from banani2017: a near-critical field phase-separates into two domains bounded by a sharp interface (variance ~7e-5 -> ~0.38 over 10000 steps), mass conserved to ~4e-18, field bounded in [-1, 1].

  • SORTING-HETEROTYPIC-INTERFACE-COLLAPSES -- hetero_frac ~0.64 -> ~0.06, roughly an order of magnitude

  • SORTING-CLUMP-STAYS-COHESIVE -- cell_pixels ~1530, <1% drift (cohesion guard, 10% bound)

  • SORTING-CONSERVES-CELL-COUNT -- n_type1 = n_type2 = 32 throughout

  • SORTING-NEUTRAL-J-DOES-NOT-SORT -- neutral J holds hetero_frac ~0.52 (J causality control)

Limitations

  • The condensate forms a boundary but does not do interface work -- it does not selectively concentrate or exclude a second species, the property that distinguishes a functional, regulatable boundary from a transient aggregate in the paper's §Molecular compositions central question (Fig 7). Named as the next step, not glossed.

  • Neither regime computes a binding affinity; sorting is differential-adhesion demixing, not molecular selectivity, matching how a coarser, specification-stage lumped realization scopes its own selectivity gap.

  • Both regimes are tuned, not fitted: the sorting J-triple satisfies Steinberg's sorting inequality (11 > 1/2*(2+2)) but the magnitudes and T=10 sit in a narrow cohesive-and-mobile window (T=1 freezes, T>=200 dissolves); the Cahn-Hilliard scheme is numerically stiff, dt pinned at 0.002. No fitted literature band is claimed because these are demonstration primitives, not models of a specific tissue or condensate.

  • The two demonstrations are independent and uncoupled; phi is not coupled to the CPM cells.

Next steps

  • Make the condensate do interface work: add a second species and a selective-partitioning coupling so a droplet demonstrably concentrates or excludes it -- turning the paper's central-question discriminator (functional regulatable boundary vs transient aggregate) into a tested pass/fail rather than a narrated one.

  • Add a genuine complementarity/selectivity term to the sorting side (a binding-affinity or partner-choice mechanism with a non-cognate binder to reject), closing the gap between complementarity-as-organizing-idea and complementarity-as-tested-mechanism.

Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • Cell sorting here is differential-adhesion demixing (Steinberg), the spatial reading of the paper's §Molecular compositions complementarity (Fig 7) -- not a molecular binding-affinity or selectivity mechanism. There is no partner-choice term, no impostor binder that could be rejected to demonstrate selectivity by contrast; cells simply minimize contact energy under a fixed J matrix. This mirrors how the specification-stage coarser, specification-stage lumped realization scopes its own selectivity gap: complementarity is the organizing idea, demixing is what is demonstrated.
  • The sorting regime is tuned, not fitted. The contact-energy triple (homotypic J=2, heterotypic J=11, medium J=8) satisfies Steinberg's sorting inequality J(1,2) > 1/2*(J(1,1)+J(2,2)) (11 > 2) -- a qualitative, theory-grounded calibration anchor -- but the magnitudes and the temperature (T=10) were chosen to sit in a narrow cohesive-and-mobile window, not fitted to a measured tissue. Outside that window the demonstration fails in two distinct ways documented in the code-verified API map's regime sweep: T=1 freezes the mixed state (no rearrangement), while T>=200 dissolves the cells into medium. No fitted literature band is claimed because these are demonstration primitives of a sorting mechanism, not a model of a specific organism's tissue.
  • The heterotypic-interface fraction is only meaningful alongside its cohesion guard. Read alone the fraction falls to 0.0 when the clump dissolves (total intercell edges -> 0, a false 'perfect sort' of an empty lattice), so the sorted claim is gated on both signals together: the fraction must drop AND the total live-cell pixel count must be retained within ~10% of its starting value. Both hold here (fraction ~0.64 -> ~0.06; pixels ~1530, <1% drift).
  • The condensate is an independent, uncoupled second demonstration. The Cahn-Hilliard field phi does not touch the CPM cells, any dFBA/cobra path, or the sorting process at all -- it is a scalar field over its own fields store. The two are separate spatial readings of complementarity-as-separation (discrete adhesion energy vs continuous composition field), deliberately not coupled; coupling phi to the cells is out of scope and unverified.
  • The Cahn-Hilliard scheme is numerically stiff and its timestep is pinned. The explicit biharmonic (nabla^4) term has an approximate stability limit dt < dx^4/(16*M*kappa); dt=0.002 (with M=1, kappa=0.5, dx=1) is verified stable over ~20000 steps, while dt=0.05 blows up to NaN within a few thousand steps -- the process raises loudly rather than silently emitting NaN. The pinned dt is a numerical calibration anchor, not a physical one.
  • Scope boundary: the condensate demonstrates phase separation (a mass-conserved boundary forming with an emergent interface), not interface work. It does not selectively concentrate or exclude a second species, which is the property that would make it a functional, regulatable boundary rather than a transient aggregate in the terms of the paper's §Molecular compositions central question (Fig 7). That is named as the next step, not glossed as already done.

Limitations

What this study explicitly does NOT validate (prevents overclaim).

  • The condensate demonstrates phase separation (a mass-conserved boundary forming with an emergent interface), not interface work: it does not selectively concentrate or exclude a second species, the property that distinguishes a functional, regulatable boundary from a transient or unregulated aggregate in the paper's §Molecular compositions central question (Fig 7). This is the honest gap and the natural next step, stated explicitly rather than glossed.
  • Neither regime computes a binding affinity. Cell sorting is differential-adhesion demixing (Steinberg), not a molecular selectivity/partner-choice mechanism -- there is no non-cognate binder that could be rejected to demonstrate selectivity by contrast, matching how a coarser, specification-stage lumped realization scopes its own selectivity gap.
  • The sorting regime is tuned, not fitted. The J-triple (2/11/8) satisfies Steinberg's sorting inequality J(1,2) > 1/2*(J(1,1)+J(2,2)) as a qualitative theory anchor, but the magnitudes and temperature (T=10) were chosen to sit in a narrow cohesive-and-mobile window (T=1 freezes the mixed state, T>=200 dissolves the cells); no fitted literature band is claimed.
  • The heterotypic-interface fraction is only valid alongside the cohesion guard -- read alone it falls to 0.0 when the clump dissolves (a false 'perfect sort'), so the sorted claim is gated on both the fraction dropping AND the live-cell pixel count being retained.
  • The Cahn-Hilliard scheme is numerically stiff; its timestep is pinned at dt=0.002 against the biharmonic stability limit dt < dx^4/(16*M*kappa). It is a diffuse-interface analogue of a condensate, not a mechanistic model of a specific biomolecular condensate.
  • The sorting and condensate demonstrations are independent and uncoupled -- phi is not coupled to the CPM cells; they are two separate spatial readings of complementarity-as-separation, deliberately not one model.
  • The deep nested-hierarchy test (an interface preserved through the deepest leaf of a many-level structural nesting) is not covered; this study spatializes two complementarity primitives (cell sorting and a condensate) but not deep structural nesting.
  • Spatializing the coupled gene-expression cascade (transcription through translation to assembly, with a ribosome pool maintained against dilution at nested depth) is future work; this study addresses only complementarity as spatial separation.
