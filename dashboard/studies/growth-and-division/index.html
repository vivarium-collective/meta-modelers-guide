Q Does growth — an autocatalytic, energy-coupled DNA-replication process that plays the role of environment-coupled uptake — drive the cell's own dna store across a threshold, and does crossing that threshold fire a genuine structural rewrite of the place graph (one cell node becoming two daughter nodes at runtime, mass conserved) rather than a pre-declared post-structure animated on a wall clock?

Claim

Growth precedes and drives division: an autocatalytic, energy-coupled DNA-replication process grows the cell's own dna store until it crosses a threshold, and crossing that threshold triggers a change to the composition itself — one cell becomes two through a graph rewrite that creates new nodes at runtime and conserves mass (the parent is halved, not doubled), not a pre-drawn pair on a timer (Law 2′, rewrite conformance vs wiring; Law 2, interface preserved across the rewrite).

Fig 10 rewrites the cell across time in two coupled moves: growth first, then division. Growth is not free — it is driven by an autocatalytic DNA-replication process, d[dna]/dt = k·dna·energy, that plays the role of environment-coupled uptake feeding the cell's internal stores (the same "sense energy from the environment, grow an internal store" pattern as Fig 5's cell-environment coupling, here specialised to DNA). This growth process is what drives dna 1 → 3.06 over the run — it is the mechanism that reaches the division threshold, not a scripted ramp. Division is the sharpest structural consequence: it is not a process nudging numbers in a fixed structure — it is a change to the place graph itself, a reaction rule in Milner's sense whose reactum has more nodes than its redex. The trigger is the cell's own grown state, not a timer: when the replicated ``dna`` crosses ``dna_threshold`` = 2.0, the rewrite fires exactly once. There is no wall clock, no ``division_time``, no scheduled tick — the cell divides because ``dna >= threshold``, and it only reaches that threshold because growth put it there. At the event the parent's biomass is partitioned, not duplicated: the parent node's biomass is driven to 0 and each of the two daughters receives M/2, so mass is conserved (one cell node → two daughter nodes). Measured on the compiled composite fig10-1-executable, the parent's ``dna`` climbs 1 → 3.06 (growth) and crosses the threshold; the rewrite fires; ``cell_count`` goes 1 → 2; each daughter inherits DNA (0 → 2.75) and half the parent's biomass (0 → 0.5) while the parent's biomass falls to 0. The daughters are structurally new — they do not exist in the initial state — which is exactly what makes this an event-driven rewrite rather than an animation of a pre-built pair.

Install a Milner reaction rule behind the Fig 10 division interface, let an autocatalytic, energy-coupled DNA-replication process grow the cell's dna to the threshold (the Fig 10 analogue of environment-coupled uptake growing an internal store), and observe the rewrite fire.

Result

The cell's replicated dna, driven by the growth process, climbs 1 → 3.06 and crosses the threshold (2.0); the single cell node is replaced by two structurally-new daughters, each inheriting dna → 2.75 and half the parent's biomass (→ 0.5) while the parent's biomass falls to 0 — a genuine, mass-conserving structural rewrite driven by the cell's own growth, not a timer.

Conclusion

Claims

  • An autocatalytic, energy-coupled DNA-replication process (growth) drives the cell's own dna store from 1 → 3.06, crossing dna_threshold = 2.0, which triggers a single division event: the cell node is replaced by two structurally-new daughter nodes and cell_count goes 1→2. The rewrite conserves mass — the parent's biomass fully partitions (parent→0, each daughter M/2=0.5), it is not duplicated. There is no wall clock: the trigger is dna >= threshold, and dna only gets there because growth put it there.

Evidence

  • GROWTH-CROSSES-THRESHOLD — the k·dna·energy growth process drives parent dna 1 → 3.06, crossing dna_threshold 2.0
  • DIVISION-FIRES — parent dna 1 → 3.06 crosses dna_threshold 2.0 → rewrite fires once, cell_count 1 → 2
  • DAUGHTERS-SPAWN — each daughter dna 0 → 2.75, biomass 0 → 0.5; parent biomass → 0 (mass conserved)
  • DRAFT-IS-INERT — sanity check: the inert draft (empty update) stays at cell_count 1 by construction

Limitations

  • Toy-real growth-and-division: the growth rate constant, DNA threshold, replication and segregation constants are illustrative; the rewrite fires once and division is symmetric.

Next steps

  • Add repeated, size-controlled, or asymmetric division rules to test the growth-and-rewrite mechanism under realistic division control.

Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • Growth is modelled by DNAReplicationODE: d[dna]/dt = k·dna·energy (k=0.15), an autocatalytic process driven by a free-energy input — the Fig 10 analogue of the environment-coupled uptake that grows internal stores elsewhere in the paper (cf. Fig 5's cell-environment coupling).
  • The division rewrite fires exactly once per run (symmetric division, fixed dna_threshold = 2.0); asymmetric division, septum placement, repeated division cycles, and size control are not modelled.

Limitations

What this study explicitly does NOT validate (prevents overclaim).

  • Symmetric division with a fixed DNA threshold; asymmetric division, septum placement, and size control are not modelled.
  • The rewrite fires once (cell_count 1→2); repeated division and spatial structure among daughters are not modelled.
  • Growth is a single autocatalytic ODE (k·dna·energy); it is not coupled to a full metabolic or spatial nutrient field in this figure.
