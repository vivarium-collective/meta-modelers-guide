Q Does the paper's §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) hold as real spatial multicellularity — multiple CPM cells sharing one diffusing nutrient field, each running its own dynamic-FBA step at its own footprint, producing both competitive exclusion and cross-feeding — when the whole colony is composed from independent frameworks through one N-cell coupling process (`CpmColonyField`), rather than the lumped two-role, shared-scalar-pool stand-in `draft-to-living-cell`'s §Cell–cell coupling study used?

Claim

Composing N real CPM cells with one shared, real spatio-flux nutrient field through a single N-cell coupling process (`CpmColonyField`) reproduces the paper's §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) as genuine spatial multicellularity, not a scripted or lumped stand-in for it: over 20-tick runs, an uptake-capacity asymmetry alone (glucose_vmax 10 vs 4) drives asymmetric growth toward competitive exclusion (biomass 237.9 vs 64.5, a 3.69x margin; volume 3511 vs 81 px — the loser displaced but still viable, above its 1.25 seed, not driven below a viability bound), while a different composite over the same coupling process — a localized glucose depot, a fast-diffusing acetate byproduct, and an acetate-consuming role — sustains a cell that never touches glucose (local glucose 0.0) purely via a diffusing cross-feeding handoff (local acetate 1.30, biomass 1.25 -> 3.79). Both regimes run through the same process; only the composite's cell roles and field constants differ, mirroring `draft-to-living-cell/cell-cell-coupling`'s law-4 pattern (two handler environments negotiate the shared-resource constraint differently over one interface) at real spatial, per-footprint metabolic resolution.

This is the spatial realization of the paper's §Cell–cell coupling pattern (no dedicated figure), built on the flagship's `CpmCellField` process generalized to `CpmColonyField` — one process instance that owns N CPM cells sharing one spatio-flux field grid, running one dynamic-FBA step per cell at its own lattice footprint each tick, and writing all N cells' uptake/secretion deltas back into the shared field as one summed update. The additive two-writer assumption this depends on (two cells' disjoint footprint deltas summing correctly into one shared `map[array]` fields store, conserving mass) is no longer an assumption — it is runtime-verified directly (`tests/test_cpm_colony_two_writer.py`): two synthetic single-pixel writers on disjoint pixels are each applied independently and sum losslessly over repeated ticks. Two tuned regimes over the same `CpmColonyField` process demonstrate both branches of the paper's §Cell–cell coupling negotiation (no dedicated figure). COMPETITION (`meta_modelers_guide.composites.cellcell-compete`): two glucose competitors seeded on a uniform 3.0 mM glucose field, differing only in uptake capacity (`glucose_vmax` 10.0 vs 4.0). Measured over a 20-tick run (`tests/test_cellcell_regimes.py::test_competition_excludes_the_slower_cell`, `tests/test_cpm_colony_field.py`): the faster competitor ends with biomass 237.9 against the slower competitor's 64.5 — a 3.69x margin — and CPM lattice volume 3511 px against 81 px, an even starker asymmetry, because CPM growth compounds superlinearly once one cell has out-competed the other for the shared glucose. The shared field is measurably drawn down by both cells drawing on the same pool, and the loser is displaced to a small fragment (81 px) of the lattice it started with parity on. This is asymmetric growth under shared-resource competition — a trend TOWARD competitive exclusion, not exclusion reached: the loser's biomass still GREW from its 1.25 seed to 64.5 (~51.6x) and stays far above any viable-biomass floor, so it is spatially DISPLACED, not driven below a viability bound (this study imposes no death and no viability constraint on a losing cell). And the winner's 3511 px is ~97.5% of the 3600-px lattice, with the field nearly exhausted by t=20 — the endpoint is as much a finite-size lattice-saturation artifact as a biological one (by t≈30 the winner fills the whole lattice and the still-viable loser is simply crowded off it, evicted rather than starved). This is exactly the paper's §Cell–cell coupling distinction that "what is coupled is not only state, but constraint": these cells couple STATE (one shared field) but no viability CONSTRAINT, so strict exclusion is out of reach here — spatial competition as a direct, unscripted consequence of two independent dFBA solves reading the same depleting field. CROSS-FEEDING (`meta_modelers_guide.composites.cellcell-crossfeed`) is the biology that carries this investigation's deepest claim: the paper's §Cell–cell coupling closes on the idea that an environmental variable acquires its significance relationally — the same molecule counts as nutrient, signal, or toxin only through its consequences for a given cell's viability. Acetate embodies exactly that here. It is the glucose-fermenting secretor's metabolic WASTE — a byproduct it dumps into the shared field — and, at the very same instant, the consumer's sole carbon FOOD, the only thing keeping it alive. One molecule, two opposite meanings, each set relationally by the cell whose interface it meets. This is textbook microbial metabolic cooperation / cross-feeding (morris2013): a byproduct of one organism's metabolism becomes an obligate resource for another, and a spatial community is knit together by the exchange. The regime realizes it concretely: a glucose-to-acetate secretor (O2 cap 5, seeded at a localized 20 mM glucose depot in lattice columns 0-18) paired with an acetate-consuming cell (`acetate_vmax` 20) seeded well outside the glucose depot (columns 22-30), with acetate diffusing far faster than glucose in this regime (diffusion coefficient 15.0 vs 0.4) so the secreted byproduct plume can reach the consumer before the glucose gradient does. Measured over the same 20-tick run (`tests/test_cellcell_regimes.py::test_crossfeeding_keeps_the_consumer_viable`): the consumer's own local glucose reads 0.0 — it genuinely cannot touch glucose — while its local acetate reads 1.30, and its biomass climbs from an initial 1.25 to 3.79 (roughly 3x) on that acetate alone, ending as a viable cell occupying 65 lattice pixels. The consumer's growth is back-loaded to the last several ticks of the run, once the secretor's plume has had time to diffuse the distance between the two cells' footprints — growth is monotonic over the window measured, not smoothly exponential from tick 0. That the secretor's acetate is what CAUSES this growth — not anything intrinsic to the consumer or the field — is closed by a secretor-knockout necessity control (`meta_modelers_guide.composites.cellcell-crossfeed-knockout`): the same composite with the secretor cell removed so NO acetate is ever produced. Run 20 ticks, the otherwise-identical consumer (now the only cell) sits at local acetate 0.0 and its biomass never climbs off its 1.25 seed — it persists on the lattice (~35 px) but flatly fails to grow, the genuine negative control for the handoff. This same acetate carries the arc across the investigation: waste-and-food here, it becomes a TOXIN in `disintegration-spatial`, where a high local acetate concentration crosses a cell's viability bound and triggers its collapse — one molecule, three relational meanings across the two studies.

Test

Result

Over 20-tick runs, the competition regime drives a decisive spatial split from uptake- capacity asymmetry alone (biomass 237.9 vs 64.5, 3.69x; CPM volume 3511 vs 81 px) -- asymmetric growth TOWARD competitive exclusion, though not exclusion reached: the loser is displaced but still viable (its biomass grew ~51.6x, far above the 1.25 seed floor), never driven below a viability bound, and the winner's ~97.5%-lattice endpoint is partly a finite-size saturation artifact -- this study couples shared state, not a viability constraint. The cross-feeding regime -- the same `CpmColonyField` process, a different composite -- sustains a consumer cell that never touches glucose (local glucose 0.0) purely via a diffusing acetate handoff (local acetate 1.30, biomass 1.25 -> 3.79, ending as a viable 65-pixel CPM cell), and a secretor-knockout control confirms that handoff is necessary (secretor removed, no acetate produced, the consumer held at its 1.25 seed). The paper's §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) holds as genuine spatial multicellularity, composed from independent frameworks through one N-cell coupling process, one interface, two regimes. The cross-feeding regime also carries the paper's closing relational-molecule claim (§Cell–cell coupling): the same acetate is metabolic waste to the secretor and sole carbon food to the consumer -- and, in `disintegration-spatial`, a toxin whose accumulation crosses a cell's viability bound -- one molecule whose meaning is set relationally by each cell's interface.

Conclusion logic — gate decision

✓ If primary tests pass:
Implementation: Both cellcell-compete.composite.json and cellcell-crossfeed.composite.json build and run to completion through the engine over 20 ticks.
Empirical validation: The spatial §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) is demonstrated at the toy-real (uncalibrated) level, with genuine per-cell CPM shape dynamics and independent dFBA solves behind both the shared-resource-competition (asymmetric growth toward exclusion; loser displaced, not excluded) and cross-feeding branches, rather than a scripted trajectory. The cross-feeding handoff is pinned as necessary by the secretor-knockout control (no acetate -> no consumer growth).
✗ If primary tests fail:
Diagnose:
  • the faster competitor's biomass/volume margin over the slower one collapses (uptake-capacity asymmetry not taking effect, or the shared field is not actually being depleted by both cells)
  • the consumer's local glucose is not near-zero (the localized glucose depot is leaking into the consumer's footprint, defeating the cross-feeding demonstration)
  • the consumer's biomass never rises despite zero local glucose (the acetate plume is not reaching it, or the consumer's acetate uptake/growth coupling is broken)
  • the secretor-knockout consumer DOES grow despite no acetate being produced (the observed cross-feeding growth was not actually caused by the secretor's acetate -- necessity broken)
  • the two-writer additivity control fails to conserve mass (the N-cell field-write assumption CpmColonyField depends on is broken)
Block downstream: future N>2-cell population-scale spatial studies, all of which reuse CpmColonyField or its coupling pattern

Conclusion

Claims

  • COMPETITION-LOSER-DISPLACED-NOT-EXCLUDED -- loser biomass 64.5 (51.6x the 1.25 viable- biomass floor): displaced (81 px vs winner 3511 px ~97.5% of the lattice), not excluded

  • CROSSFEEDING-CONSUMER-GROWS-ON-ACETATE-ALONE -- consumer biomass 1.25 -> 3.79 on acetate alone
  • CROSSFEEDING-ACETATE-PLUME-REACHES-CONSUMER (positive check) -- consumer local_acetate == 1.30 by t=20
  • CROSSFEEDING-SECRETOR-KNOCKOUT-CONSUMER-FAILS-TO-GROW (negative control) -- secretor removed, no acetate produced, consumer biomass held at its 1.25 seed (vs 1.25 -> 3.79)

Limitations

  • "Competitive exclusion" is not literally reached over the run: the loser is displaced (81 px, biomass 64.5) but stays viable -- this study couples shared state, not a viability constraint, so no cell is driven below a viability bound (§Cell–cell coupling: what is coupled is not only state, but constraint -- here, only state). The winner's 3511-px (~97.5% of 3600) endpoint is partly a finite-size lattice-saturation artifact; at t≈30 the winner fills the lattice and the still-viable loser is crowded off it (eviction, not starvation).
  • e_coli_core-derived constants (glucose_vmax, acetate_vmax, grow_per_biomass, the O2 cap) are toy-real -- plausible, hand-tuned magnitudes, not fitted to any organism or medium.
  • The cross-feeding regime's acetate/glucose diffusion ratio (15.0 vs 0.4) is directionally defensible (acetate is a smaller molecule) but was chosen, not fitted; the localized glucose depot is a deliberately engineered spatial arrangement, not a naturally arising gradient.
  • The consumer's growth under cross-feeding is back-loaded to roughly the last 5 ticks of the run, once the acetate plume has had time to diffuse across the distance between the two cells' footprints -- monotonic over the measured window, not smoothly exponential from tick 0.
  • Each CPM cell gets its own independent cobra model copy, not one shared model instance across the colony.
  • CPM-native chemotaxis (directed motion toward a sensed gradient) remains out of scope for both regimes here.

Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • e_coli_core-derived constants (`glucose_vmax` 10.0/4.0 for the two competitors, `acetate_vmax` 20 for the consumer, `grow_per_biomass` 30, the O2 cap of 5 on the secretor) are toy-real: plausible, hand-tuned magnitudes chosen to make competitive exclusion and cross- feeding both legible over a short (20-tick) run, not fitted to a named organism or medium.
  • The cross-feeding regime's acetate/glucose diffusion ratio (15.0 vs 0.4) is directionally defensible — acetate is a smaller molecule and diffuses faster than glucose in real aqueous media — but the specific ratio was chosen to make the byproduct plume legibly reach the consumer within a 20-tick run, not fitted to measured diffusion coefficients. Likewise the cross-feeding regime's localized 20 mM glucose depot (columns 0-18 only, with the consumer seeded outside it at columns 22-30) is a deliberately engineered spatial arrangement chosen so the consumer genuinely sees zero glucose (local_glucose == 0.0), not a naturally arising gradient.
  • The consumer's growth under cross-feeding is back-loaded to roughly the last 5 ticks of the 20-tick run, once the secretor's acetate plume has had time to diffuse across the distance separating the two cells' footprints — growth is monotonic over the measured window but is not a smooth exponential from tick 0, and a shorter run would show less of it.
  • Each CPM cell in `CpmColonyField` gets its own independent cobra model copy, not one shared model instance across the colony — avoiding any risk of one cell's dFBA solve silently perturbing another's solver state, at the cost of N times the per-tick FBA solve cost.
  • The additive two-writer assumption `CpmColonyField` depends on — that disjoint per-cell footprint deltas can be summed into one shared `map[array]` fields store and conserve mass — is no longer taken on faith; it is runtime-verified directly by `tests/test_cpm_colony_two_writer.py` against two synthetic single-pixel writers on disjoint pixels, independently of the colony process itself.
  • CPM-native chemotaxis (directed motion up a sensed gradient, e.g. the consumer moving toward the secretor's acetate plume rather than merely sitting in its path) remains out of scope for both regimes here, matching the flagship's own deferral.
  • The "viable-biomass floor" this study measures the competition loser against is the 1.25 seed biomass every cell starts at: a cell above its seed biomass is metabolically viable (net- growing or holding), one that has fallen below it is in net mass-loss toward death. This is a deliberately minimal, toy-real viability notion, not a fitted maintenance/decay bound; the process emits no explicit viability flag, so viability is read off the per-cell biomass observable. The point it establishes is honest and robust to the exact floor value: the competition loser ends at 64.5 (51.6x the floor), so it is displaced, not excluded.
  • The competition regime reaches NO true competitive exclusion over the run measured — it imposes no death and no viability constraint on a losing cell, so the loser (biomass 64.5, 81 px) is spatially displaced, not eliminated. The winner's 3511-px endpoint is ~97.5% of the 3600-px lattice with the field nearly spent by t=20, so that endpoint is partly a finite-size lattice-saturation artifact; extending the run to t≈30 fills the lattice and crowds the still- viable loser off it (eviction, not starvation). Strict exclusion would require coupling a viability constraint, not only shared state — the paper's §Cell–cell coupling distinction.
  • MULTI-SEED ROBUSTNESS (the headline is a range, not a single-seed point). The competition headline ratio (3.69x, id 1 / id 2 biomass) is a SINGLE-SEED reading of a stochastic CPM Metropolis trajectory. A 5-seed sweep (`tests/test_cellcell_multiseed.py`, seeds 1-5, CPM seed varied via the now config-overridable `CpmColonyField` seed) gives the honest range: ratio 2.9x-3.7x, mean ~3.2x -- the hard-coded single-seed 3.69x is the TOP of the band, not its middle. The qualitative claim is seed-robust: the faster competitor (id 1, glucose_vmax 10) wins on both biomass and lattice volume at every seed, always by > 1.5x. This study establishes the multi-seed convention for the investigation (process reads `seed` from config; deep-copy the composite state, sweep the seed, assert the qualitative claim per seed and report the numeric range); studies 5/7/9 copy the pattern rather than resting on a single hard-coded seed.
  • The secretor-knockout necessity control (`cellcell-crossfeed-knockout`) removes the secretor cell entirely rather than only silencing its acetate secretion in place; this is the cleanest single-variable knockout (no acetate source anywhere) and avoids a confound of the alternative (raising the secretor's O2 cap so it stops overflowing also makes it grow far faster and crowd the consumer off the lattice). The consumer is therefore the only cell in the control (id 1), and its glucose exchange stays off by role, so the slowly-diffusing glucose depot it cannot eat is harmless to the control.

Limitations

What this study explicitly does NOT validate (prevents overclaim).

  • "Competitive exclusion" is not literally reached: the competition loser is spatially displaced (ends at 81 px, biomass 64.5 -- still 51.6x above its 1.25 seed / viable-biomass floor) but is never driven below a viability bound. This study couples shared STATE (one field) but no viability CONSTRAINT (no death, no viability bound on a losing cell), so the honest phenomenon is asymmetric growth TOWARD exclusion, not exclusion. The winner's 3511-px endpoint is ~97.5% of the 3600-px lattice with the field nearly exhausted by t=20, so that endpoint is partly a finite-size lattice-saturation artifact; extending the run to t≈30 fills the lattice and crowds the still-viable loser off it (eviction, not starvation), which a viability-constraint coupling would be needed to turn into genuine exclusion.
  • The competition headline ratio (3.69x biomass, id 1 / id 2) is a single-seed number; a 5-seed robustness sweep (tests/test_cellcell_multiseed.py) puts the honest range at ~2.9x-3.7x (mean ~3.2x), with the single-seed 3.69x at the top of the band and the faster competitor winning every seed by > 1.5x. See key_assumptions for the convention this establishes for the investigation.
  • e_coli_core-derived constants (glucose_vmax, acetate_vmax, grow_per_biomass, the O2 cap) are toy-real, hand-tuned magnitudes chosen to make both regimes legible over a short run -- not fitted to any organism or medium.
  • The cross-feeding regime's acetate/glucose diffusion ratio (15.0 vs 0.4) is directionally defensible -- acetate is a smaller molecule and diffuses faster than glucose in real aqueous media -- but the specific ratio was chosen to make the plume legibly reach the consumer within 20 ticks, not fitted to measured diffusion coefficients; the localized 20 mM glucose depot is likewise a deliberately engineered spatial arrangement so the consumer genuinely sees zero glucose, not a naturally arising gradient.
  • The consumer's growth under cross-feeding is back-loaded to roughly the last 5 ticks of the 20-tick run, once the secretor's acetate plume has had time to diffuse across the distance separating the two cells' footprints -- monotonic over the measured window, but not smoothly exponential from tick 0.
  • Each CPM cell in CpmColonyField gets its own independent cobra model copy, not one shared model instance across the colony.
  • The additive two-writer assumption CpmColonyField's N-cell field-write depends on is now runtime-verified directly (tests/test_cpm_colony_two_writer.py), independent of both regimes' own dFBA mechanism -- previously an unverified assumption inherited from the flagship study.
  • The secretor-knockout necessity control removes the secretor CELL entirely (cleanest single- variable knockout: no acetate source anywhere) rather than silencing acetate secretion in place; the alternative -- raising the secretor's O2 cap so it stops overflowing -- also makes it grow far faster and crowd the consumer off the lattice, a confound the cell-removal avoids. The control therefore establishes that acetate is necessary for the consumer's growth, but not the finer counterfactual of an in-place, same-growth secretor that merely stops secreting.
