Q The paper's §Development and evolution section (Fig 10c-f) extends the interface idea across time: individual cellular interfaces become embedded in a COLLECTIVE interface defined by shared state -- a growing colony develops physiological heterogeneity (a depleted core against a fed rim) where each cell's own interface sits inside one collective interface -- and a heritable trait EVOLVES when variation is selected on that shared field. This study puts both halves on the real dividing CPM colony and asks the discriminating question directly: does a growing CPM colony on a shared depleting glucose field form emergent core-vs-rim heterogeneity (DEVELOPMENT), and does a heritable per-cell glucose-uptake trait (vmax) shift its population mean UP under shared-field selection (EVOLUTION) -- and, crucially, is that directional shift caused by SELECTION rather than mutation alone? The design is a DUAL control: against the selection-ON colony, a no-mutation arm (the trait cannot vary) and a no-selection arm (the trait mutates but confers no fitness) test whether selection, not mutation or drift, drives it. This study REUSES study 8's dividing colony (`growth-and-division`, `CpmGrowthDivision`) unchanged and adds ONLY one heritable coupling parameter -- the paper's claim that the interfaces and couplings are themselves evolvable features of organization, made concrete by making one coupling parameter heritable and selectable on the colony that already grows and divides.

Claim

The paper's §Development and evolution section (Fig 10c-f) holds as a spatial mechanism on the real dividing CPM colony, REUSING study 8's `CpmGrowthDivision` unchanged and adding only a heritable per-cell coupling parameter. DEVELOPMENT: a growing colony on a shared depleting glucose field forms emergent core-vs-rim physiological heterogeneity -- the radial rim-core glucose ratio is an ENSEMBLE BAND across N=30 seeds (mean 1.283, sd 0.159, range [1.062, 1.815]; flagship seed 3 ends 1.445; every seed clears the 1.0 floor), a collective interface not imposed but emerging from shared-field competition. EVOLUTION: a heritable glucose-uptake trait (vmax) shifts its population mean UP under shared-field selection, and at N=30 seeds SELECTION is a STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT driver -- a Mann-Whitney U rank test on the per-seed deltas (selection-ON vs no-selection) gives U=685, p=5.3e-4 (rank-biserial r=+0.52, CLES 0.76), and the drift-null check rejects neutral drift (Wilcoxon p=1.2e-5); selection-ON mean delta +0.233 (sd 0.296, up 22/30). A DUAL control isolates selection -- the no-mutation control stays exactly at the founder value with zero variance (0/30, static, no raw material), and the no-selection control mutates (var > 0) but drifts undirected near zero (-0.026, up 13/30, the neutral-drift envelope). The directional up-shift appears only under selection AND is distinguishable from drift at p<0.001, so the dual control ESTABLISHES selection (not mutation, not drift) via differential division on the shared field (higher-vmax cells grow/divide more, so their trait proliferates) -- upgrading the earlier n=5 "consistent with" reading; the effect is medium and the shift modest (+0.23 over ~4 generations). The gate is re-leveled onto this ensemble rank test, off the flagship seed 3 (kept only as a regression pin). The boundaries are stated as findings: the development and evolution halves pull the same field-depletion knob and both saturate within the ~gen 4-6 crowding window; the scope is a toy evo-devo (development = shared-field core/rim heterogeneity, NOT morphogenesis; evolution = one scalar trait under resource selection, NOT genome evolution; facilitated variation -- evolving the ports/couplings themselves -- is a named deferral).

The paper's §Development and evolution section (Fig 10c-f) is where the cellular-interface idea is extended across developmental and evolutionary time, and it is the hardest pair in the paper -- a coarser, non-spatial lumped realization could only gesture at it, its own verdict conceding that its "selection" was a single ODE with fixed viability/fitness constants and its "new port" a scripted config ramp: pattern, not phenomenon. This study is the capstone of the nine spatial studies and supplies exactly the phenomenon that one named as missing, at a very different mechanistic resolution, by REUSING the study-8 dividing colony rather than building anything new. DEVELOPMENT -- the collective interface, measured. A CPM colony grows on a shared 60x60 glucose field: each cell runs its own per-footprint dynamic-FBA (dFBA against e_coli_core) to grow and divide (study 8's mechanism, unchanged). As the colony compounds, the interior cells sit inside the colony's own depleted core while the outer cells are fed from the rim -- so each cell's individual interface (its local glucose exchange) becomes embedded in a COLLECTIVE interface defined by the shared field. This is measured radially: binning live cells by distance from the colony centroid (median split into core/rim) and comparing mean local glucose, the rim-core ratio (`rim_core_ratio`) grows from 1.0 (few cells, no readable gradient) to ~1.44 by the end of a 45-tick run -- the core is measurably more depleted than the rim. Nothing imposes this: it is the same shared-field mass-balance that produced study 8's division desync, now read as a spatial gradient -- physiological heterogeneity EMERGING from shared-field competition (stewart2008/flemming2016 biofilm physiological heterogeneity), not a patterning program. The rim-core ratio is now an ENSEMBLE BAND across N=30 seeds (mean 1.283, sd 0.159, range [1.062, 1.815]; the flagship seed 3 ends 1.445), with the per-cell core/rim local-glucose split recorded as the scatter behind the scalar -- every seed clears the 1.0 no-gradient floor, so the gradient is an ensemble result, not a single-seed illustration. EVOLUTION -- one heritable trait, selected on the shared field. Each cell carries a heritable scalar trait, its glucose-uptake `vmax`, used as that cell's own dFBA uptake parameter. On division the daughter inherits the parent's trait plus a Gaussian mutation (sigma=0.3, seeded RNG) through the SAME creation-order parent<->daughter pairing study 8 already uses for biomass and lineage; the parent keeps its own trait. Selection is emergent and mechanistic: a higher-vmax cell draws more glucose, grows faster, crosses `vol_threshold` sooner, divides more, and so its trait proliferates -- selection through DIFFERENTIAL DIVISION on the shared depleting field, not an externally-imposed fitness function. Measured across an N=30-seed ensemble (45 ticks, founder vmax0=1.5): the population mean `vmax` shifts UP with mean delta +0.233 (sd 0.296, up in 22/30 seeds) -- and, tested against the no-selection neutral-drift envelope, the shift is STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT (Mann-Whitney U=685, p=5.3e-4; drift-null Wilcoxon p=1.2e-5). THE DUAL CONTROL isolates selection as the driver, and at N=30 seeds the isolation is now STATISTICALLY ESTABLISHED. Two negative controls, each identical to the selection-ON colony but for one flag. NO-MUTATION (`mutate: false`, a BOOLEAN flag -- never a `mut_sigma: 0.0` float override, which bigraph_schema silently drops): the trait cannot vary, so the population mean stays EXACTLY the founder value 1.500 with zero variance in every seed (0/30 shift, static) -- no raw material for selection to act on. NO-SELECTION (`selection: false`): the trait still mutates (`var_vmax > 0`, raw variance builds) but every cell's dFBA uses the fixed founder vmax0 regardless of its own trait, so the trait confers no fitness -- the mean drifts UNDIRECTED near zero (mean delta -0.026, up in 13/30 seeds), the neutral-drift envelope. Comparing the two arms across N=30 seeds: a Mann-Whitney U rank test on the per-seed deltas is significant (U=685, p=5.3e-4, rank-biserial r=+0.52, CLES 0.76 -- a random selection-ON seed exceeds a random no-selection seed 76% of the time), and a drift-null check (Wilcoxon of the selection-ON deltas above the no-selection median) rejects neutral drift at p=1.2e-5. So the dual control now ESTABLISHES selection (not mutation, not drift) as the cause -- upgrading the earlier under-powered n=5 reading (4/5 vs 2/5, not then significant). The effect is medium and the shift modest, and the re-gate is on this ensemble rank test rather than the flagship seed 3. The absence of variation in the no-mutation arm (0/30) shows heritable variation is necessary; the directional up-shift being present only under selection, and distinguishable from drift, is the positive evidence that selection is the driver. What is demonstrated, and what is not. FIRST, this is a strict UPGRADE over the non-spatial study 9: real heritable variation and real fitness-based selection (differential division on a shared field) replace that study's empty variation handler and fixed-constant ODE. SECOND, the two halves pull the same knob: both development heterogeneity and evolution intensify with stronger field depletion, and the colony saturates its own footprint on the 60x60 lattice by roughly generation 4-6, so both are demonstrated within that bounded window -- a larger grid or a leaner field would run more generations and sharpen the gradient, at the cost of the same crowding that drives the heterogeneity. THIRD, the scope is a TOY evo-devo: DEVELOPMENT here is shared-field-driven core/rim physiological heterogeneity, NOT morphogenesis, signalling, or a genetic patterning program; EVOLUTION is ONE scalar heritable trait under resource selection, NOT genome or regulatory-network evolution and NOT speciation. kirschner2005 "facilitated variation" -- evolving the interfaces/couplings THEMSELVES (the ports and wiring), not a parameter of a fixed port -- is a named deferral: this study mutates a parameter of a fixed coupling, exactly the axis the non-spatial study's reviewer said would be needed to move from pattern to phenomenon.

Test

Conclusion

Claims

  • DEVELOPMENT holds spatially: a growing CPM colony on a shared depleting glucose field forms emergent core-vs-rim physiological heterogeneity -- rim_core_ratio is an ENSEMBLE BAND across N=30 seeds (mean 1.283, sd 0.159, range [1.062, 1.815]; flagship seed 3 ends 1.445; every seed clears the 1.0 floor), a collective interface not imposed but emerging from shared-field competition (the paper's §Development and evolution section, Fig 10c-d).

  • EVOLUTION holds spatially, and at N=30 seeds SELECTION is a STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT driver: a Mann-Whitney U rank test on the per-seed mean-vmax deltas (selection-ON vs no-selection) gives U=685, p=5.3e-4 (rank-biserial r=+0.52, CLES 0.76), and the drift-null check rejects neutral drift (Wilcoxon of the selection-ON deltas above the no-selection median, p=1.2e-5). Selection-ON mean delta +0.233 (up 22/30); the no-mutation control stays exactly at the founder (0/30, static -- no raw material) and the no-selection control drifts undirected near zero (-0.026, up 13/30 -- the neutral-drift envelope). The DUAL control now ESTABLISHES selection (not mutation, not drift) as the cause of the directional shift, upgrading the earlier under-powered n=5 reading; the effect is medium and the shift modest (+0.23 mean over ~4 generations). The gate is re-leveled onto this ensemble rank test, off the flagship seed 3 (kept only as a regression pin).

  • This REUSES study 8's CpmGrowthDivision unchanged and adds only one heritable coupling parameter -- the paper's "interfaces and couplings are themselves evolvable features of organization," made concrete.

Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • This study REUSES study 8's dividing colony (`CpmGrowthDivision`, `growth-and-division`) UNCHANGED and adds ONLY a heritable per-cell trait plus the dev/evo observables. `CpmEvolution` is a minimal subclass wired through the base's two no-op hooks: `_cell_glucose_vmax(cid)` (returns this cell's heritable `vmax` instead of the population-wide constant) and `_on_division(parent, daughter)` (draws the daughter's trait from the parent + Gaussian mutation). The base's dFBA-growth + native `divide_cells` + biomass-partition + lineage loop is called verbatim via `super().update()`; nothing in the growth/division mechanism is duplicated or altered. This is the paper's "interfaces and couplings are themselves evolvable features of organization" made concrete -- one heritable coupling parameter added to the existing colony.
  • DEVELOPMENT is MEASURED, not imposed. `rim_core_ratio` bins live cells by Euclidean distance from the colony centroid (median split into core <= median, rim > median) and compares mean `local_glucose` between the two groups; rim/core > 1 means the core is more depleted than the rim. No structure is written into the field or the cells -- the gradient is the same shared-field mass-balance that produced study 8's division desync, now read radially. It starts at 1.0 (too few cells to split, or no readable gradient) and grows to ~1.44 as the colony develops, an emergent collective interface (stewart2008/flemming2016 biofilm physiological heterogeneity).
  • EVOLUTION selection is EMERGENT and MECHANISTIC, not an imposed fitness function. The trait `vmax` IS the cell's own dFBA glucose-uptake parameter, so a higher-vmax cell draws more glucose, grows faster, crosses `vol_threshold` sooner, divides more, and its trait proliferates -- selection through DIFFERENTIAL DIVISION on the shared depleting field. The trait shift and the fact that selected populations reach more cells are the SAME mechanism (selection acting through division rate), not two independent findings.
  • The DUAL control isolates selection, and both controls are built to avoid two verified config traps. NO-MUTATION is a BOOLEAN flag (`mutate: false`), never a `mut_sigma: 0.0` float override -- bigraph_schema silently drops a `0.0` float override (schema default retained), so a float-gated "no mutation" arm would secretly keep mutating and masquerade as weak selection. NO-SELECTION (`selection: false`) keeps mutation ON but makes every cell's dFBA use the fixed founder `vmax0` regardless of its trait, so variation is present but confers no fitness. Only with BOTH controls in place does the selection-ON directional shift isolate selection (not mutation, not drift) as the cause.
  • EVOLUTION is stochastic and reported as an N=30-seed ENSEMBLE with a rank test, not a single-seed headline. The `seed` config is threaded into BOTH the CPM potts spec (world construction -- the base process hardcodes `potts.seed=1`, so without this every "different seed" replays one Metropolis trajectory) AND the mutation RNG, so the ensemble is deterministic and reproducible. Across N=30 seeds selection-ON shifts the mean up in 22/30 seeds (mean delta +0.233); the reported result is the Mann-Whitney U rank test of selection-ON vs no-selection deltas (p=5.3e-4) plus a drift-null check, extending the multi-seed convention established on `cell-cell-coupling` (`tests/test_cellcell_multiseed.py`) to a powered significance test (scripts/run_dev_evo_ensemble.py, tests/test_dev_evo_ensemble.py).
  • The two halves pull the SAME knob (field depletion), and the demonstration is bounded by crowding. Both development heterogeneity and evolution intensify with stronger field depletion, and the colony saturates the 60x60 lattice by roughly generation 4-6, so both are demonstrated WITHIN that bounded window. A larger grid or leaner field would run more generations and sharpen the gradient -- at the cost of the same crowding that itself drives the heterogeneity. The constants are toy-real (`vmax0` 1.5, `mut_sigma` 0.3, `vol_threshold` 80, `reset_target` 40, `mcs` 3, T 11 carried from study 8), not fitted to any organism; no literature acceptance band is claimed.
  • The SCOPE is a toy evo-devo. DEVELOPMENT = shared-field-driven core/rim physiological heterogeneity, NOT morphogenesis, signalling, or a genetic patterning program (the collective interface is a measured resource gradient, not a regulated body plan). EVOLUTION = ONE scalar heritable trait (glucose `vmax`) under resource selection, NOT genome/regulatory-network evolution and NOT speciation. kirschner2005 "facilitated variation" -- evolving the ports/couplings THEMSELVES rather than a parameter of a fixed port -- is a NAMED DEFERRAL: this study mutates a parameter of a fixed coupling.
