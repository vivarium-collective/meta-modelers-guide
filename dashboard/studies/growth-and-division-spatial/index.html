Q A cell grows, crosses a threshold, and divides -- and its daughters do not necessarily divide together. This is the paper's §Growth and division growth-then-divide pattern (Fig 10a,b): metabolism drives a cell's own state across a threshold, crossing it fires division and partitions state across the two resulting daughters, and those daughters, "remain[ing] coupled through shared environmental state," can either stay in step or diverge (§Growth and division). Does this hold spatially -- a single CPM cell whose metabolism (dFBA solved at its own lattice footprint) grows its volume until it crosses `vol_threshold` and divides via the native CPM engine operation `divide_cells`, partitioning biomass mass-conserved across the two daughters, compounding into a small multi-generation lineage tree -- and do sibling cells sharing one glucose field begin growing at different local rates as the colony crowds, so their division timing desynchronizes rather than staying locked to clean powers of two? Composed from independent frameworks (viva-cpm + spatio-flux + cobra) through one coupling process (`CpmGrowthDivision`).

Claim

Metabolism-driven growth crossing a threshold fires division, and division partitions state -- biomass, and lattice pixels -- across two daughters that resume growing themselves, compounding into a genuine multi-generation lineage tree whose siblings desynchronize as they compete for shared, local resources: this is the paper's §Growth and division growth-then-divide pattern (Fig 10a,b), including its "coordination and divergence" clause, realized spatially. One real CPM cell, growing via real per-footprint dFBA metabolism, divides via the native CPM engine operation `divide_cells` (biomass partitioned proportional to post-split daughter volume, not reset and discarded) through a single coupling process (`CpmGrowthDivision`). Over a 36-tick run sampled every 3 ticks: the founder compounds into an 18-cell lineage tree spanning 4 generations (`max_generation` = 4), the population steps up in a monotonic non-decreasing staircase (1,2,2,2,4,4,5,8,8,11,14,18) that departs from clean powers of two from tick 21 onward as sibling cells' division timing diverges, and every cell's CPM volume stays bounded in a sawtooth between `reset_target` = 40 and `vol_threshold` = 80. The staircase is bounded more by lattice crowding (the colony packs to roughly two-thirds occupancy within its own footprint) than by nutrient limitation (field-wide glucose depletes only ~3% over the run). This is direct simulation output from three independently-developed frameworks (viva-cpm + spatio-flux + cobra) meeting at one typed interface. It mirrors `draft-to-living-cell/growth-and-division`'s §Growth and division claim (Fig 10a,b) (growth drives the cell's own state across a threshold; crossing the threshold triggers division) but realizes division as the native spatial CPM operation `divide_cells` acting directly on lattice pixels, in contrast to that study's place-graph rewrite (one node becoming two structurally-new nodes at a lumped, non-spatial level of composition).

A cell metabolizes, grows, crosses a threshold, and divides -- passing its state on to two daughters that each resume growing themselves. This is the paper's §Growth and division growth-then-divide pattern (Fig 10a,b): growth drives a cell's own state across a threshold, crossing it fires division, and division "partitions state variables such as DNA and biomass into two cells" (§Growth and division, Fig 10b caption) rather than discarding them. Here one CPM cell seeded on a uniform, abundant 60x60 glucose field (`glucose_vmax` deliberately tuned down to 1.5, `vol_threshold` 80, `reset_target` 40) grows its volume from real per-footprint dFBA metabolism against `e_coli_core` (the same metabolism-drives-growth mechanism as the flagship, `cell-environment-coupling-spatial`), and once its CPM volume crosses `vol_threshold`, the native engine operation `divide_cells(vol_threshold, reset_target)` splits it into two mass-conserved daughters (parent keeps its id, one new id is minted), partitions the parent's tracked biomass across them proportional to their post-split lattice volumes -- not resetting both to a fixed value, which would destroy accumulated biomass -- and resets both to `reset_target` physical volume so growth resumes in each. Mechanistically this composes one CPM cell (viva-cpm), a shared spatio-flux glucose field, and cobra's `e_coli_core` FBA solver through a single coupling process, `CpmGrowthDivision`. The native division primitive this depends on is not an assumption -- it is runtime-verified directly (`tests/test_cpm_divide_spike.py`): a single grown cell split at threshold 80/reset 40 yields exactly one new daughter id, the parent id is kept, and the two daughters' combined volume matches the pre-split volume within rounding; a cell below threshold is confirmed a no-op. Measured over a 36-tick run sampled every 3 ticks (`tests/test_growth_division_regime.py`, `tests/test_cpm_growth_division.py`, `tests/test_growth_division_viz.py`): the founder compounds into an 18-cell lineage tree spanning 4 full generations -- founder id 1 (generation 0) divides into ids 2, 3, 5, and 10 (generation 1: 4 cells), whose own descendants populate generation 2 (7 cells) and generation 3 (4 cells), reaching ids 16 and 17 at generation 4 (2 cells) by the end of the run (`max_generation` = 4). This is a genealogy, not a population count: every live cell carries its own `generation` and parent id (`lineage`), recorded from the engine's own `divide_cells` return value, not scripted. The population steps up 1 -> 2 -> 2 -> 2 -> 4 -> 4 -> 5 -> 8 -> 8 -> 11 -> 14 -> 18, never losing a cell to a phantom or a runaway single cell absorbing the colony, and per-cell CPM volume sawtooths in a bounded band (roughly 31-79 px) throughout the run as each cell grows toward `vol_threshold`, is halved by division back down near `reset_target`, and immediately resumes growing. The lineage does not stay in lockstep. The staircase departs from clean powers of two starting at tick 21 (5 cells, not 4 or 8) and stays desynchronized for the rest of the run (11, 14, 18 -- none a power of two): sibling cells, coupled through the shared glucose field, grow at different local rates as the colony crowds -- a cell on the fed rim of the growing colony out-competes one boxed into a depleting interior -- so their division timing diverges even though they share a common parent. By the final sampled tick, cells spanning all five generations (0 through 4) coexist simultaneously in the same 18-cell colony -- founder id 1 (generation 0 by definition, since generation counts ancestry depth, not divisions performed) is still alive in the colony alongside some of its great-great-grandchildren, which have already reached generation 4. This is exactly the paper's own language for what happens right after division: "these systems remain coupled through shared environmental state, so their interfaces are not independent ... enabling both coordination and divergence" (§Growth and division) -- and it is the first emergent multicellular heterogeneity this investigation demonstrates: not a nuisance the staircase survives, but the phenomenon itself. HONEST caveat on what actually bounds the staircase: with `glucose_vmax` = 1.5 on an abundant uniform field, field-wide glucose barely dips -- roughly 3% over the full 36-tick run (43180 -> 41826 arbitrary field units) -- so nutrient depletion is a weak brake, not the dominant one. What visibly slows and desynchronizes division is lattice crowding: by the end of the run the 18-cell colony packs to roughly two-thirds occupancy within its own bounding footprint (936 px of live cell volume in a ~1458 px colony bounding box), even though the full 60x60 lattice remains only ~26% occupied overall -- cells pushing against their immediate neighbors, not starving for glucose. The claim is therefore "bounded by lattice crowding and, more weakly, shared-glucose depletion," not "bounded by the shared glucose field" as an earlier framing of this study put it.

Conclusion logic — gate decision

✓ If primary tests pass:
Implementation: growth-division-spatial.composite.json builds and runs to completion through the engine over a 36-tick run.
Empirical validation: The spatial §Growth and division growth-then-divide pattern (Fig 10a,b) is demonstrated at the toy-real (uncalibrated) level, with genuine per-footprint dFBA metabolism driving growth, the native CPM divide_cells operation firing division at threshold and partitioning biomass across daughters, and a real tracked lineage (not a scripted one) whose siblings desynchronize under shared-field competition, per the paper's "coordination and divergence."
✗ If primary tests fail:
Diagnose:
  • the population never steps up past 1 (glucose_vmax too low or vol_threshold never reached, or divide_cells is not being called)
  • n_cells ever decreases (a division is losing a cell, or a duplicate/phantom id is being dropped)
  • per-cell volume runs away past 200 or collapses toward 0 (glucose_vmax tuned too high, re-crossing vol_threshold almost every tick and crowding the lattice; or reset_target is being applied incorrectly)
  • the native divide_cells control fails to conserve mass or fails the no-op check (the engine primitive this study's division depends on is broken, independent of the composite)
  • the staircase stays a clean power of two the whole run (siblings would be growing in perfect lockstep, meaning the shared-field coupling is not actually creating local heterogeneity), or max_generation never compounds past 1-2 (the lineage bookkeeping is broken or division is not recursing into daughters)
Block downstream: any future spatial study reusing the CpmGrowthDivision pattern or the native divide_cells primitive (e.g. a spatial development-and-evolution study that needs a growing, dividing colony)

Conclusion

Claims

  • Metabolism-driven growth crossing a threshold fires division, and division partitions state across two daughters that resume growing themselves, compounding into a genuine multi-generation lineage tree whose siblings desynchronize as they compete for shared, local resources -- the paper's §Growth and division growth-then-divide pattern (Fig 10a,b), including its "coordination and divergence" clause, realized spatially by one real CPM cell, real per-footprint dFBA metabolism, and the native CPM engine operation divide_cells through a single coupling process (CpmGrowthDivision). Over a 36-tick run sampled every 3 ticks: the founder compounds into an 18-cell lineage tree spanning 4 generations (max_generation = 4), the population steps up in a monotonic non-decreasing staircase (1,2,2,2,4,4,5,8,8,11,14,18) that departs from clean powers of two from tick 21 onward, and every cell's CPM volume stays bounded in a sawtooth between reset_target = 40 and vol_threshold = 80. The staircase is bounded more by lattice crowding than by nutrient limitation (field-wide glucose depletes only ~3% over the run).

Evidence

  • POPULATION-STEPS-UP-BY-DIVISION -- n_cells trajectory 1,2,2,2,4,4,5,8,8,11,14,18 over 36 ticks
  • POPULATION-NEVER-SHRINKS -- n_cells trajectory is monotonic non-decreasing
  • DAUGHTERS-RESUME-GROWTH-WITHOUT-RUNAWAY -- n_cells >= 3 after 30 ticks from a single seed cell, all volumes in (5, 200)
  • PER-CELL-VOLUME-STAYS-BOUNDED -- per-cell volume bounded roughly 31-79 px across the run
  • NATIVE-DIVISION-CONSERVES-MASS -- divide_cells splits one cell into two mass-conserved daughters; no-op below threshold, verified independently
  • DIVISION-DESYNCHRONIZES-FROM-LOCKSTEP -- n_cells departs from powers of two from tick 21 onward (5, 11, 14, 18); 5 distinct generations coexist in the colony at the final tick
  • LINEAGE-COMPOUNDS-INTO-A-TREE -- max_generation reaches 4; every daughter's generation traces its recorded parent's generation + 1

Limitations

  • Growth is genuinely metabolism-driven (real dFBA against e_coli_core at the cell's own footprint) but the constants (glucose_vmax = 1.5, glucose_km, box_volume_L, grow_per_biomass, oxygen_vmax) are toy-real -- plausible, hand-tuned magnitudes, not fitted to any organism or medium.
  • Division is the native CPM engine operation divide_cells -- a modeling primitive triggered by the volume threshold, splitting the cell (approximately across its long axis) and conserving lattice-pixel mass between daughters -- NOT emergent membrane constriction or cytokinesis mechanics. The trigger is genuine simulation state; the split itself is an engine-provided operation. The process's own tracked biomass is partitioned proportional to post-split daughter volume (not reset to a fixed value), per §Growth and division's Fig 10b "partitions state variables such as DNA and biomass."
  • The run spans roughly 3-4 generations (1 -> 2 -> 4 -> 8 -> 18 cells), not by a hard-coded generation limit. Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • Growth is genuinely metabolism-driven -- a real dynamic-FBA solve against `e_coli_core` at the cell's own CPM footprint each tick, the same mechanism as the flagship's `CpmCellField` -- but the constants (`glucose_vmax` = 1.5, `glucose_km`, `box_volume_L`, `grow_per_biomass`, `oxygen_vmax`) are toy-real: plausible, hand-tuned magnitudes chosen to make the growth-divide staircase legible over a 36-tick run, not fitted to a named organism or medium.
  • Division is the NATIVE CPM engine operation `divide_cells(vol_threshold, reset_target)` -- a modeling primitive triggered by the cell's own grown volume crossing `vol_threshold`, which splits the cell (approximately across its long axis) into two daughters, conserves lattice-pixel mass between them, and resets both daughters' physical target volume to `reset_target` -- NOT emergent membrane constriction or cytokinesis mechanics. The trigger condition (crossing a volume threshold reached by real metabolism-driven growth) is genuine simulation state; the split operation itself is an engine-provided primitive, not a simulated physical process. The process's own tracked `biomass` -- which is what drives future growth via `set_target_volume` -- is handled separately from the engine's physical reset: per the paper (§Growth and division, Fig 10b caption, division "partitions state variables such as DNA and biomass into two cells"), the parent's pre-division biomass is split between the two daughters proportional to their post-division lattice volumes, mass-conserving, not reset to a fixed value that would discard it. This mirrors the honest framing `disintegration-spatial` gave its own resorption primitive: a chosen, code-verified engine operation standing in for a mechanism (cytokinesis) the underlying CPM engine does not itself simulate.
  • The run spans roughly 3-4 generations (1 -> 2 -> 4 -> 8 -> 18 cells), not by any hard-coded generation limit. `glucose_vmax` is deliberately tuned down from the brief's literal reference value of 10.0 to 1.5: at 10.0 every live cell grows fast enough to re-cross `vol_threshold` almost every tick, and the population explodes past the lattice's pixel budget within ~20 ticks, crowding cells down toward near-zero-volume phantoms. At 1.5, growth is slow enough that the staircase stays legible and every cell settles in a bounded volume band between divisions. What actually bounds the staircase, measured: field-wide glucose barely dips (~3% over 36 ticks), so nutrient depletion is a weak brake; the dominant limit is lattice crowding -- the 18-cell colony packs to roughly two-thirds occupancy within its own bounding footprint even though the full 60x60 lattice remains only ~26% occupied overall. "Bounded by the shared glucose field" overstates the nutrient contribution; "bounded by lattice crowding and, more weakly, shared-glucose depletion" is the honest claim.
  • Each CPM cell gets its own independent cobra model copy, not one shared model instance across the lineage -- the same convention as `cell-cell-coupling-spatial`'s `CpmColonyField` -- keyed off the live cell ids read from the engine's own snapshot each tick, so newly divided daughters are picked up automatically without scripted lineage bookkeeping.
  • The native `divide_cells` primitive this study's division depends on is no longer an assumption; it is runtime-verified directly (`tests/test_cpm_divide_spike.py`) against a synthetic single-cell world, independently of the growth-division composite's own metabolism mechanism: parent id kept, one new daughter id minted, combined post-split volume matches the pre-split volume within rounding, and a sub-threshold cell is confirmed a no-op.
  • Read alongside its specification-stage analogue, `draft-to-living-cell/growth-and-division`: that study drives an autocatalytic ODE (`d[dna]/dt = k*dna*energy`) to grow the cell's own dna store past `dna_threshold`, then fires division as a place-graph rewrite -- a structural change to the composition itself, one cell node becoming two structurally-new daughter nodes, with the parent's biomass halved rather than duplicated. This study drives growth with real per-footprint dFBA metabolism instead of a lumped ODE, and realizes division as the native spatial CPM operation `divide_cells` acting directly on lattice pixels instead of a graph rewrite. Compare the two for the same growth-then-divide claim at two very different mechanistic and compositional resolutions -- place-graph rewrite vs. spatial pixel-level engine operation.
