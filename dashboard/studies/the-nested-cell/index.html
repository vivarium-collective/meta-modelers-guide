Q How do molecules compose into a cell? Can the Fig 8 molecular composition be authored as a deeply nested place graph — membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, chromosome, chromatin, nucleosome — with a gene-expression cascade wired to its deepest leaves, and does compilation preserve the interface even six levels down?

Claim

Molecular composition is a nested containment hierarchy, and an interface can be preserved even when it is wired to the deepest levels of that nesting.

Fig 8 is the inside view: a cell as a hierarchy of compartments, each containing the next. The place graph nests six levels deep — extracellular matrix outside the membrane, cytoplasm within, nucleus within that, then chromosome, chromatin, and finally the nucleosome that holds the DNA. Wired through this structure is the central dogma cascade: DNA is transcribed to RNA (rna → 1.41), RNA is translated to protein (proteins → 0.52), ribosomes assemble (0.5 → 0.63), and metabolism feeds it (nutrients → 2.4, energy → 0.84) across the membrane transport port (0.3). The compiler must preserve every port at every depth: the deepest leaf, nucleosome.DNA, rises from 1.0 to 1.4, proving the interface survives the deepest nesting.

Test

Build the Fig 8 six-level place graph (ECM → membrane → cytoplasm → nucleus → chromosome → nucleosome) and wire a transcription–translation cascade through it.

Model nested-hierarchy
Measures protein-expressed·mrna-transcribed·Negative control: the inert draft (no mechanism) MUST fail this.

Result

The cascade reaches steady states in the right order — mRNA fast, protein lagging — with the interface preserved at the deepest leaf.

Conclusion

Claims

  • The six-level nested place graph carries a central-dogma cascade WITH degradation that reaches steady states in the correct time hierarchy — mRNA settles fast (≈2.4), protein lags (≈10.5), metabolite slowest (≈20) — the transcription-before-translation ordering.

Evidence

  • STEADY-STATES — mRNA≈2.4, protein≈10.5, metabolite≈20 (steady)
  • TIME-HIERARCHY — mRNA fast, protein lags, metabolite slowest
  • CASCADE-FLOWS — all four levels populated
  • conservation — time hierarchy: mRNA settles fast, protein lags — mRNA_ss≈2.40, protein_ss≈10.48

Limitations

  • Physically-consistent TOY model: quantities are conserved and rate-law shapes are real (Monod, Michaelis–Menten, Fick, Arrhenius, logistic), but constants are illustrative, not fitted to a specific organism.

Next steps

  • Parameterize the rate constants from literature to move from correct-shape to quantitatively-calibrated.
