Q Can multicellular development (Fig 10) be expressed as compositional reorganization — individual cells attaching, secreting extracellular matrix, and assembling into a biofilm that is itself a higher-level composite with its own aggregate observables?

Claim

Development is composition at a higher level: individual cells reorganize into a community that is itself a composite with its own aggregate behavior.

If division makes two cells from one, development makes a community from many. Fig 10-2 treats a biofilm as a composite at a level above the single cell: cells adhere to a surface and grow logistically toward a carrying capacity (cells 0.2 → 5.0 = K), secrete a shared extracellular matrix (ecm accumulating to ≈ 21.8), and the whole assembly acquires aggregate, colony-level observables that no single cell has. The compositional point is that the same modelling apparatus that describes one cell describes their collective by nesting: the biofilm is a store containing cells, with processes that act on the group.

Test

Compose cells with attachment, ECM secretion, and logistic growth behind the Fig 10 development interface and run to carrying capacity.

Model development-rewrite, development
Measures colony-growth·ecm-production·Negative control: the inert draft (no mechanism) MUST fail this.

Result

The colony saturates at its surface carrying capacity while matrix accumulates — a bounded, resource-limited community, not unbounded growth.
