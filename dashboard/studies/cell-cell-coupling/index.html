Q Does the paper's §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) hold as real spatial multicellularity — multiple CPM cells sharing one diffusing nutrient field, each running its own dynamic-FBA step at its own footprint, producing both resource preemption between two cells and cross-feeding — when the whole colony is composed from independent frameworks through one N-cell coupling process (`CpmColonyField`), rather than the lumped two-role, shared-scalar-pool stand-in a lumped, specification-stage realization used?

Claim

Composing N real CPM cells with one shared, real spatio-flux nutrient field through a single N-cell coupling process (`CpmColonyField`) reproduces the paper's §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) as genuine spatial multicellularity, not a scripted or lumped stand-in for it: over 20-tick runs, an uptake-capacity asymmetry alone (glucose_vmax 10 vs 4) drives asymmetric growth via resource preemption between the two cells (biomass ratio 2.9-3.7x across 5 seeds, 3.69x on the flagship seed; volume 3511 vs 81 px — the loser displaced but still viable, above its 1.25 seed, not driven below a viability bound; the dividing-population lift (M7b, `cellcell-compete-div`) turns this into robust competitive DOMINANCE — fast lineage ~9x the slow's biomass, monoculture-controlled — with full exclusion only stochastic, so exclusion is earned only as a seed-dependent outcome, not deterministically, and the winner's ~97.5%-lattice swell without division is a saturation artifact), while a different composite over the same coupling process — a localized glucose depot, a fast-diffusing acetate byproduct, and an acetate-consuming role — sustains a cell that never touches glucose (local glucose 0.0) purely via a diffusing cross-feeding handoff (local acetate 1.30, biomass 1.25 -> 3.79). Both regimes run through the same process; only the composite's cell roles and field constants differ, mirroring the lumped ODE predecessor's law-4 pattern (two handler environments negotiate the shared-resource constraint differently over one interface) at real spatial, per-footprint metabolic resolution.

This is the spatial realization of the paper's §Cell–cell coupling pattern (no dedicated figure), built on the flagship's `CpmCellField` process generalized to `CpmColonyField` — one process instance that owns N CPM cells sharing one spatio-flux field grid, running one dynamic-FBA step per cell at its own lattice footprint each tick, and writing all N cells' uptake/secretion deltas back into the shared field as one summed update. The additive two-writer assumption this depends on (two cells' disjoint footprint deltas summing correctly into one shared `map[array]` fields store, conserving mass) is no longer an assumption — it is runtime-verified directly (`tests/test_cpm_colony_two_writer.py`): two synthetic single-pixel writers on disjoint pixels are each applied independently and sum losslessly over repeated ticks. Two tuned regimes over the same `CpmColonyField` process demonstrate both branches of the paper's §Cell–cell coupling negotiation (no dedicated figure). COMPETITION (`meta_modelers_guide.composites.cellcell-compete`): two glucose competitors seeded on a uniform glucose field (3.0 model units), differing only in uptake capacity (`glucose_vmax` 10.0 vs 4.0). Measured over a 20-tick run (`tests/test_cellcell_regimes.py::test_competition_excludes_the_slower_cell`, `tests/test_cpm_colony_field.py`): the faster competitor ends with biomass 237.9 against the slower competitor's 64.5 — a 3.69x margin — and CPM lattice volume 3511 px against 81 px, an even starker asymmetry, because CPM growth compounds superlinearly once one cell has out-competed the other for the shared glucose. The shared field is measurably drawn down by both cells drawing on the same pool, and the loser is displaced to a small fragment (81 px) of the lattice it started with parity on. This is asymmetric growth under shared-resource competition — resource preemption between two cells, not population-level competitive exclusion (which would require dividing populations): the loser's biomass still GREW from its 1.25 seed to 64.5 (~51.6x) and stays far above any viable-biomass floor, so it is spatially DISPLACED, not driven below a viability bound (this study imposes no death and no viability constraint on a losing cell). And the winner's 3511 px is ~97.5% of the 3600-px lattice, with the field nearly exhausted by t=20 — the endpoint is as much a finite-size lattice-saturation artifact as a biological one (a single cell swelling to ~97.5% of the lattice without ever dividing is a lattice artifact, not physiology; by t≈30 the winner fills the whole lattice and the still-viable loser is simply crowded off it, evicted rather than starved). This is exactly the paper's §Cell–cell coupling distinction that "what is coupled is not only state, but constraint": these cells couple STATE (one shared field) but no viability CONSTRAINT, so strict exclusion is out of reach here — spatial competition as a direct, unscripted consequence of two independent dFBA solves reading the same depleting field. M7b (peer review) lifts exactly this to DIVIDING founder lineages with a viability floor (`cellcell-compete-div`, CpmCompeteDivision) and finds robust population-level competitive DOMINANCE — the fast lineage holds ~9x the slow lineage's biomass by t=40, and the slow lineage stays viable in monoculture (`cellcell-compete-div-slowmono`) so its collapse is genuinely competition-driven (Tilman R*). Full competitive EXCLUSION (slow lineage → 0) is only stochastic here — it lands at the headline seed and in 2/5 sweep seeds, while elsewhere the suppressed slow lineage persists in spatial refugia — so even with dividing populations the earned term is DOMINANCE, not deterministic exclusion. CROSS-FEEDING (`meta_modelers_guide.composites.cellcell-crossfeed`) is the biology that carries this investigation's deepest claim: the paper's §Cell–cell coupling closes on the idea that an environmental variable acquires its significance relationally — the same molecule counts as nutrient, signal, or toxin only through its consequences for a given cell's viability. Acetate embodies exactly that here. It is the glucose-fermenting secretor's metabolic WASTE — a byproduct it dumps into the shared field — and, at the very same instant, the consumer's sole carbon FOOD, the only thing keeping it alive. One molecule, two opposite meanings, each set relationally by the cell whose interface it meets. This is textbook microbial metabolic cooperation / cross-feeding (morris2013): a byproduct of one organism's metabolism becomes an obligate resource for another, and a spatial community is knit together by the exchange. The regime realizes it concretely: a glucose-to-acetate secretor (O2 cap 5, seeded at a localized glucose depot (20 model units) in lattice columns 0-18) paired with an acetate-consuming cell (`acetate_vmax` 20) seeded well outside the glucose depot (columns 22-30), with acetate diffusing far faster than glucose in this regime (diffusion coefficient 15.0 vs 0.4) so the secreted byproduct plume can reach the consumer before the glucose gradient does. Measured over the same 20-tick run (`tests/test_cellcell_regimes.py::test_crossfeeding_keeps_the_consumer_viable`): the consumer's own local glucose reads 0.0 — it genuinely cannot touch glucose — while its local acetate reads 1.30, and its biomass climbs from an initial 1.25 to 3.79 (roughly 3x) on that acetate alone, ending as a viable cell occupying 65 lattice pixels. The consumer's growth is back-loaded to the last several ticks of the run, once the secretor's plume has had time to diffuse the distance between the two cells' footprints — growth is monotonic over the window measured, not smoothly exponential from tick 0. That the secretor's acetate is what CAUSES this growth — not anything intrinsic to the consumer or the field — is closed by a secretor-knockout necessity control (`meta_modelers_guide.composites.cellcell-crossfeed-knockout`): the same composite with the secretor cell removed so NO acetate is ever produced. Run 20 ticks, the otherwise-identical consumer (now the only cell) sits at local acetate 0.0 and its biomass never climbs off its 1.25 seed — it persists on the lattice (~35 px) but flatly fails to grow, the genuine negative control for the handoff. This same acetate carries the arc across the investigation: waste-and-food here, it becomes a TOXIN in `disintegration`, where a high local acetate concentration crosses a cell's viability bound and triggers its collapse — one molecule, three relational meanings across the two studies.

Result

Over 20-tick runs, the competition regime drives a decisive spatial split from uptake- capacity asymmetry alone (biomass ratio 2.9-3.7x across 5 seeds, 3.69x on the flagship seed; 237.9 vs 64.5; CPM volume 3511 vs 81 px) -- asymmetric growth via resource preemption between the two cells, not population-level competitive exclusion in this baseline: the loser is displaced but still viable (its biomass grew ~51.6x, far above the 1.25 seed floor), never driven below a viability bound, and the winner's ~97.5%-lattice endpoint is partly a finite-size saturation artifact -- the baseline couples shared state, not a viability constraint. Peer-review M7b lifts this to DIVIDING founder lineages with a viability floor (`cellcell-compete-div`) and measures the real outcome -- robust competitive DOMINANCE (fast lineage ~9x the slow's biomass by t=40, competition-driven per the `cellcell-compete-div-slowmono` monoculture control) with full exclusion only stochastic (headline seed and 2/5 sweep seeds; the suppressed slow lineage otherwise persists in spatial refugia) -- so the earned term is dominance, not deterministic exclusion. The cross-feeding regime -- the same `CpmColonyField` process, a different composite -- sustains a consumer cell that never touches glucose (local glucose 0.0) purely via a diffusing acetate handoff (local acetate 1.30, biomass 1.25 -> 3.79, ending as a viable 65-pixel CPM cell), and a secretor-knockout control confirms that handoff is necessary (secretor removed, no acetate produced, the consumer held at its 1.25 seed). Peer-review M7a re-runs that handoff at the physical 2:1 acetate/glucose diffusivity (`cellcell-crossfeed-realistic`) and finds it FAILS (consumer local_acetate ~0.21 vs 1.60, biomass ~1.28 vs 3.79, then displaced), with feeding scaling monotonically with transport acceleration -- so the flagship's accelerated transport is load-bearing for the legible handoff at this geometry, not cosmetic. The paper's §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) holds as genuine spatial multicellularity, composed from independent frameworks through one N-cell coupling process, one interface, two regimes. The cross-feeding regime also carries the paper's closing relational-molecule claim (§Cell–cell coupling): the same acetate is metabolic waste to the secretor and sole carbon food to the consumer -- and, in `disintegration`, a toxin whose accumulation crosses a cell's viability bound -- one molecule whose meaning is set relationally by each cell's interface.

Conclusion

Claims

  • Composing N real CPM cells with one shared, real spatio-flux nutrient field through a single N-cell coupling process (CpmColonyField) reproduces the paper's §Cell–cell coupling viability-negotiation pattern (no dedicated figure) as genuine spatial multicellularity. Over 20-tick runs: an uptake-capacity asymmetry alone (glucose_vmax 10 vs 4) drives asymmetric growth via resource preemption between the two cells (biomass ratio 2.9-3.7x across 5 seeds, 3.69x on the flagship seed; 237.9 vs 64.5; volume 3511 vs 81 px) -- the loser is spatially displaced but stays viable (its biomass grew ~51.6x above the 1.25 seed floor), not driven below a viability bound; this is resource preemption between two single, never-dividing cells, not population-level competitive exclusion (which would need dividing populations), and the winner's ~97.5%-lattice swell without division is a finite-size saturation artifact; a different composite over the same process -- localized glucose depot, fast-diffusing acetate byproduct, acetate-consuming role -- sustains a cell that never touches glucose (local glucose 0.0) purely via a diffusing cross-feeding handoff (local acetate 1.30, biomass 1.25 -> 3.79), and a secretor-knockout control shows that handoff is necessary (no acetate -> consumer held at its 1.25 seed).

Evidence

  • COMPETITION-EXCLUDES-THE-SLOWER-CELL -- biomass 237.9 (id 1) vs 64.5 (id 2), 3.69x margin
  • COMPETITION-VOLUME-TRACKS-BIOMASS -- CPM volume 3511 px (id 1) vs 81 px (id 2)
  • COMPETITION-LOSER-DISPLACED-NOT-EXCLUDED -- loser biomass 64.5 (51.6x the 1.25 viable- biomass floor): displaced (81 px vs winner 3511 px ~97.5% of the lattice), not excluded
  • CROSSFEEDING-CONSUMER-BLIND-TO-GLUCOSE -- consumer local_glucose == 0.0
  • CROSSFEEDING-CONSUMER-GROWS-ON-ACETATE-ALONE -- consumer biomass 1.25 -> 3.79 on acetate alone
  • CROSSFEEDING-ACETATE-PLUME-REACHES-CONSUMER (positive check) -- consumer local_acetate == 1.30 by t=20
  • CROSSFEEDING-SECRETOR-KNOCKOUT-CONSUMER-FAILS-TO-GROW (negative control) -- secretor removed, no acetate produced, consumer biomass held at its 1.25 seed (vs 1.25 -> 3.79)
  • TWO-WRITER-ADDITIVITY-CONSERVES-MASS (structural check) -- disjoint per-cell field writes sum losslessly, runtime-verified

Limitations

  • "Competitive exclusion" is not literally reached in the BASELINE regime: the loser is displaced (81 px, biomass 64.5) but stays viable -- the baseline couples shared state, not a viability constraint, so no cell is driven below a viability bound (§Cell–cell coupling: what is coupled is not only state, but constraint -- here, only state). The winner's 3511-px (~97.5% of 3600) endpoint is partly a finite-size lattice-saturation artifact; at t≈30 the winner fills the lattice and the still-viable loser is crowded off it (eviction, not starvation). M7b (peer review) lifts this to dividing lineages with a viability floor (cellcell-compete-div) and finds robust competitive DOMINANCE (fast ~9x slow, monoculture-controlled) with full exclusion only stochastic -- so even there the earned term is dominance, not deterministic exclusion.
  • The BASELINE cross-feed's fast acetate transport (37.5x) is a legibility choice; M7a (cellcell-crossfeed-realistic) shows it is load-bearing -- at the physical 2:1 diffusivity the handoff FAILS (consumer biomass ~1.28 vs 3.79) before the consumer is competitively displaced.
  • e_coli_core-derived constants (glucose_vmax, acetate_vmax, grow_per_biomass, the O2 cap) are toy-real -- plausible, hand-tuned magnitudes, not fitted to any organism or medium.
  • The cross-feeding regime's acetate/glucose diffusion ratio (15.0 vs 0.4 = 37.5x) is a legibility choice, not a calibration: acetate does diffuse faster, but the real aqueous ratio is only ~1.5-2x, so 37.5x is roughly 20x the physical ratio (chosen, not fitted); the localized glucose depot is a deliberately engineered spatial arrangement, not a naturally arising gradient.
  • The consumer's growth under cross-feeding is back-loaded to roughly the last 5 ticks of the run, once the acetate plume has had time to diffuse across the distance between the two cells' footprints -- monotonic over the measured window, not smoothly exponential from tick 0.
  • Each CPM cell gets its own independent cobra model copy, not one shared model instance across the colony.
  • CPM-native chemotaxis (directed motion toward a sensed gradient) remains out of scope for both regimes here.

Next steps

  • Extend CpmColonyField to N > 2 cells to test whether the competitive/cross-feeding pattern generalizes to population-scale spatial negotiation.
  • Add CPM-native chemotaxis so the consumer can move toward the sensed acetate plume rather than merely sitting in its path.
  • Fan out to the next spatial counterpart in the planned increments.

Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • e_coli_core-derived constants (`glucose_vmax` 10.0/4.0 for the two competitors, `acetate_vmax` 20 for the consumer, `grow_per_biomass` 30, the O2 cap of 5 on the secretor) are toy-real: plausible, hand-tuned magnitudes chosen to make resource preemption and cross- feeding both legible over a short (20-tick) run, not fitted to a named organism or medium.
  • The cross-feeding regime's acetate/glucose diffusion ratio (15.0 vs 0.4 = 37.5x) is a legibility choice, not a calibration: acetate does diffuse faster than glucose (it is a smaller molecule), but the real aqueous ratio is only ~1.5-2x (glucose ≈ 600 µm²/s, acetate ≈ 1100 µm²/s), so 37.5x is roughly 20x the physical ratio. The specific ratio was chosen to make the byproduct plume legibly reach the consumer within a 20-tick run, not fitted to measured diffusion coefficients. M7a (peer review) now MEASURES what that choice buys — re-run at the physical 2:1 ratio (`cellcell-crossfeed-realistic`), the handoff FAILS (consumer local_acetate peaks ~0.21 vs 1.60, biomass ~1.28 vs 3.79, then the consumer is displaced), and consumer feeding scales monotonically with transport acceleration (+14%/+45%/+102%/+203% at 5x/10x/20x/37.5x). So the accelerated transport is LOAD-BEARING for the legible handoff at this 6-cell-gap / 20-tick geometry, not a cosmetic relabeling. Likewise the cross-feeding regime's localized glucose depot (20 model units) (columns 0-18 only, with the consumer seeded outside it at columns 22-30) is a deliberately engineered spatial arrangement chosen so the consumer genuinely sees zero glucose (local_glucose == 0.0), not a naturally arising gradient.
  • The consumer's growth under cross-feeding is back-loaded to roughly the last 5 ticks of the 20-tick run, once the secretor's acetate plume has had time to diffuse across the distance separating the two cells' footprints — growth is monotonic over the measured window but is not a smooth exponential from tick 0, and a shorter run would show less of it.
  • Each CPM cell in `CpmColonyField` gets its own independent cobra model copy, not one shared model instance across the colony — avoiding any risk of one cell's dFBA solve silently perturbing another's solver state, at the cost of N times the per-tick FBA solve cost.
  • The additive two-writer assumption `CpmColonyField` depends on — that disjoint per-cell footprint deltas can be summed into one shared `map[array]` fields store and conserve mass — is no longer taken on faith; it is runtime-verified directly by `tests/test_cpm_colony_two_writer.py` against two synthetic single-pixel writers on disjoint pixels, independently of the colony process itself.
  • CPM-native chemotaxis (directed motion up a sensed gradient, e.g. the consumer moving toward the secretor's acetate plume rather than merely sitting in its path) remains out of scope for both regimes here, matching the flagship's own deferral.
  • The "viable-biomass floor" this study measures the competition loser against is the 1.25 seed biomass every cell starts at: a cell above its seed biomass is metabolically viable (net- growing or holding), one that has fallen below it is in net mass-loss toward death. This is a deliberately minimal, toy-real viability notion, not a fitted maintenance/decay bound; the process emits no explicit viability flag, so viability is read off the per-cell biomass observable. The point it establishes is robust to the exact floor value: the competition loser ends at 64.5 (51.6x the floor), so it is displaced, not excluded.
  • The BASELINE competition regime (`cellcell-compete`) is resource preemption between two single, never-dividing cells, NOT population-level competitive exclusion. It imposes no death and no viability constraint on a losing cell, so the loser (biomass 64.5, 81 px) is spatially displaced, not eliminated. The winner's 3511-px endpoint is ~97.5% of the 3600-px lattice with the field nearly spent by t=20 — a single cell swelling to fill the lattice without dividing is a finite-size saturation artifact, not physiology; extending the run to t≈30 fills the lattice and crowds the still-viable loser off it (eviction, not starvation). M7b (peer review) supplies the missing ingredients — DIVIDING founder lineages PLUS a viability floor that removes a starved cell (`cellcell-compete-div`, CpmCompeteDivision) — and measures the real outcome — robust population-level competitive DOMINANCE (fast lineage ~9x the slow's biomass by t=40), competition-driven (the slow lineage is viable in monoculture, `cellcell-compete-div-slowmono`), with full competitive EXCLUSION only stochastic (headline seed and 2/5 sweep seeds; elsewhere the suppressed slow lineage persists in spatial refugia). So even coupling a viability constraint AND dividing populations, strict deterministic exclusion is NOT robustly reached at this geometry — the earned vocabulary is competitive DOMINANCE, not exclusion — the paper's §Cell–cell coupling distinction that what is coupled is not only state but constraint.
  • MULTI-SEED ROBUSTNESS (the headline is a range, not a single-seed point). The competition headline ratio (3.69x, id 1 / id 2 biomass) is a SINGLE-SEED reading of a stochastic CPM Metropolis trajectory. A 5-seed sweep (`tests/test_cellcell_multiseed.py`, seeds 1-5, CPM seed varied via the now config-overridable `CpmColonyField` seed) gives the range: ratio 2.9x-3.7x, mean ~3.2x -- the hard-coded single-seed 3.69x is the TOP of the band, not its middle. The qualitative claim is seed-robust: the faster competitor (id 1, glucose_vmax 10) wins on both biomass and lattice volume at every seed, always by > 1.5x. This study establishes the multi-seed convention for the investigation (process reads `seed` from config; deep-copy the composite state, sweep the seed, assert the qualitative claim per seed and report the numeric range); studies 5/7/9 copy the pattern rather than resting on a single hard-coded seed.
  • The secretor-knockout necessity control (`cellcell-crossfeed-knockout`) removes the secretor cell entirely rather than only silencing its acetate secretion in place; this is the cleanest single-variable knockout (no acetate source anywhere) and avoids a confound of the alternative (raising the secretor's O2 cap so it stops overflowing also makes it grow far faster and crowd the consumer off the lattice). The consumer is therefore the only cell in the control (id 1), and its glucose exchange stays off by role, so the slowly-diffusing glucose depot it cannot eat is harmless to the control.

Limitations

What this study explicitly does NOT validate (prevents overclaim).

  • The BASELINE regime (`cellcell-compete`) is resource preemption between two single, never-dividing cells, NOT population-level competitive exclusion: the competition loser is spatially displaced (ends at 81 px, biomass 64.5 -- still 51.6x above its 1.25 seed / viable-biomass floor) but is never driven below a viability bound. This study couples shared STATE (one field) but no viability CONSTRAINT (no death, no viability bound on a losing cell), so the baseline phenomenon is resource preemption, not exclusion. The winner's 3511-px endpoint is ~97.5% of the 3600-px lattice with the field nearly exhausted by t=20 -- a single cell swelling to fill the lattice without dividing is a finite-size saturation artifact, not physiology. M7b (peer review, `cellcell-compete-div`) adds exactly the missing ingredients -- DIVIDING founder lineages PLUS a viability floor that removes a starved cell -- and finds robust population-level competitive DOMINANCE (fast lineage ~9x the slow's biomass by t=40, competition-driven per the monoculture control) with full EXCLUSION only stochastic (headline seed and 2/5 sweep seeds; the suppressed slow lineage otherwise persists in spatial refugia). So even with a viability constraint AND dividing populations, strict deterministic exclusion is NOT robustly reached at this geometry -- the earned term is competitive DOMINANCE, not exclusion.
  • The competition headline is a multi-seed band, not a single-seed point: a 5-seed robustness sweep (tests/test_cellcell_multiseed.py) puts the biomass ratio at ~2.9x-3.7x (mean ~3.2x), with the single-seed flagship 3.69x at the TOP of the band, and the faster competitor winning every seed by > 1.5x. See key_assumptions for the convention this establishes for the investigation.
  • e_coli_core-derived constants (glucose_vmax, acetate_vmax, grow_per_biomass, the O2 cap) are toy-real, hand-tuned magnitudes chosen to make both regimes legible over a short run -- not fitted to any organism or medium.
  • The cross-feeding regime's acetate/glucose diffusion ratio (15.0 vs 0.4 = 37.5x) is a legibility choice, not a calibration -- acetate does diffuse faster than glucose (smaller molecule), but the real aqueous ratio is only ~1.5-2x (glucose ≈ 600 µm²/s, acetate ≈ 1100 µm²/s), so 37.5x is roughly 20x the physical ratio, chosen to make the plume legibly reach the consumer within 20 ticks, not fitted to measured diffusion coefficients; the localized glucose depot (20 model units) is likewise a deliberately engineered spatial arrangement so the consumer genuinely sees zero glucose, not a naturally arising gradient. M7a (peer review, `cellcell-crossfeed-realistic`) MEASURES the cost of that choice -- re-run at the physical 2:1 ratio the handoff FAILS (consumer local_acetate peaks ~0.21 vs 1.60, biomass ~1.28 vs 3.79, then the consumer is displaced), and feeding scales monotonically with transport acceleration (+14%/+45%/+102%/+203% at 5x/10x/20x/37.5x) -- so the accelerated transport is load-bearing for the legible handoff at this geometry, not cosmetic.
  • The consumer's growth under cross-feeding is back-loaded to roughly the last 5 ticks of the 20-tick run, once the secretor's acetate plume has had time to diffuse across the distance separating the two cells' footprints -- monotonic over the measured window, but not smoothly exponential from tick 0.
  • Each CPM cell in CpmColonyField gets its own independent cobra model copy, not one shared model instance across the colony.
  • The additive two-writer assumption CpmColonyField's N-cell field-write depends on is now runtime-verified directly (tests/test_cpm_colony_two_writer.py), independent of both regimes' own dFBA mechanism -- previously an unverified assumption inherited from the flagship study.
  • The secretor-knockout necessity control removes the secretor CELL entirely (cleanest single- variable knockout: no acetate source anywhere) rather than silencing acetate secretion in place; the alternative -- raising the secretor's O2 cap so it stops overflowing -- also makes it grow far faster and crowd the consumer off the lattice, a confound the cell-removal avoids. The control therefore establishes that acetate is necessary for the consumer's growth, but not the finer counterfactual of an in-place, same-growth secretor that merely stops secreting.
  • CPM-native chemotaxis (directed motion up a sensed gradient) remains out of scope for both regimes here, matching the flagship's own deferral.
  • The BASELINE shared nutrient field has no continuous supply term; the competition regime depletes a fixed initial mass, producing a finite-lattice-saturation artifact (the winner ends near full-lattice while the field is nearly exhausted). M7b (peer review, `cellcell-compete-div`) removes this confound -- a chemostat-like boundary glucose supply (Dirichlet BC) plus dividing populations and a viability floor -- and tests population-level competition at a sustained resource level rather than transient depletion; the result is robust competitive DOMINANCE (full exclusion only stochastic), reported above.
  • The compete, crossfeed, and knockout composites are asserted in prose to share the same external colony interface, but this is not verified by a structural check; a diagnostic test asserting interface equality across the mechanism swaps would strengthen the substrate-independence claim from assertion to demonstration.
