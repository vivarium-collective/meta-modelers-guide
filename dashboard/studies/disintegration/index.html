Q Does the paper's §Disintegration level-shift (Fig 5) hold spatially -- a coherent CPM cell that loses structural viability when a diffusing field of acetate (the SAME metabolic byproduct that is waste-and-food in `cell-cell-coupling`, here toxic at high local concentration) crosses its viability bound, its domain resorbing while its shed material becomes scattering physical particles -- composed from independent frameworks (viva-cpm + spatio-flux particles) through one coupling process (`CpmDisintegration` + `BrownianMovement`)?

Claim

Composing one real CPM cell with a real spatio-flux acetate field and a real spatio-flux particle-movement process through two coupling processes (`CpmDisintegration` for the viability-collapse trigger and resorption, stock `BrownianMovement` for the debris scatter) reproduces the paper's §Disintegration level-shift (Fig 5) as spatial dissolution with a SCRIPTED resorption schedule (resorb_per_tick=6.0): the trigger timing is simulation-derived (the footprint-local acetate crossing a viability bound), the resorption ramp itself is scripted -- not a lumped scalar stand-in: over a fixed-seed deterministic 20-tick CPM run, the cell holds coherent (area > 30) through tick 6, the footprint-local acetate mean crosses viability_threshold at released_tick = 7, the domain resorbs to area 0 by tick 16, and its 63 shed pixels become a debris cloud whose RMS spread strictly increases through tick 24 (the trajectory -- hold, cross, resorb, particle count -- reproduces exactly; the scatter cloud moves under unseeded `BrownianMovement`, so its exact RMS magnitudes are representative of one run, not run-to-run-reproducible -- only the strict-increase direction is the robust claim) -- hold, cross, resorb, scatter, end to end from one coupling process plus one stock movement process, mirroring a lumped, specification-stage realization's §Disintegration level-shift pattern (Fig 5) at real spatial, per-pixel resolution.

This is the spatial realization of the paper's §Disintegration pattern (Fig 5), built on a new coupling process, `CpmDisintegration`, that owns a single CPM cell (no metabolism/cobra path -- this study is about the viability-collapse trigger, not nutrient uptake) sitting on a shared spatio-flux `acetate` field. The stressor species here is literally `acetate` (the process reads it via config `stressor_field: acetate`; the observable stays named `mean_stressor`) -- the SAME molecule that is the secretor's metabolic waste and the consumer's sole carbon food in `cell-cell-coupling`. This is the paper's closing §Disintegration/§Cell–cell coupling claim in action: an environmental variable acquires its significance relationally, a molecule counting as nutrient, signal, or toxin through its consequences for viability. The same acetate that keeps a cross-feeding consumer alive elsewhere becomes, at high local concentration, the toxin that pushes THIS cell across its viability bound. Each tick the process reads mean acetate over the cell's own CPM footprint; once that reading crosses `viability_threshold`, it LATCHES a `released` flag (never un-crosses) and ramps the cell's target volume toward zero, shedding each tick's vacated footprint pixels as debris particles into a shared `map[particle]` store that a seeded `SeededBrownianMovement` (a seed-adding subclass of spatio-flux `BrownianMovement`) then scatters. The CPM-to-particle bridge this depends on -- a process emitting particles via the map `_add` sentinel that a separate stock movement process then moves -- is no longer an assumption; it is runtime-verified directly (`tests/test_particle_bridge_spike.py`): three synthetic particles emitted on one tick are still exactly three particles four ticks later, with at least two of them having moved. One tuned regime over `disintegration-spatial` (`meta_modelers_guide.composites.disintegration-spatial`) demonstrates the full arc: a single CPM cell centered on a 60x60 shared field grid inside a radial acetate gradient -- low at the grid center where the cell is seeded, rising toward the periphery -- diffusing under spatio-flux `DiffusionAdvection`. The CPM trajectory -- hold, cross, resorb -- is fixed-seed deterministic and reproduces exactly run to run (measured over a 20-tick run, `tests/test_cpm_disintegration.py`, `tests/test_disintegration_regime.py`, `tests/test_disintegration_viz.py`, verified over repeated runs): the cell holds coherent (area > 30 pixels) through tick 6 with zero debris, the footprint-local acetate mean (the observable `mean_stressor`) crosses `viability_threshold` at `released_tick = 7`, and from there the domain resorbs monotonically -- area 56 at tick 7 down to area 0 by tick 16 -- while shedding exactly 63 particles into the debris store (up to 8/tick, capped, until the footprint is exhausted). Every one of those numbers -- `released_tick`, the area trajectory, and the 63-particle shed count -- is reproducible exactly, because the CPM engine is fixed-seed and the shedding logic is a deterministic function of the footprint diff. The debris cloud's scatter is now deterministic too: it moves under a seeded `SeededBrownianMovement` (seed=1, a seed-adding subclass of the stock spatio-flux process, since the stock one draws from NumPy's global RNG), so the RMS-spread values below are now exactly reproducible run to run -- `tests/test_disintegration_seed.py` asserts byte-identical debris positions across two builds -- and the qualitative claim (RMS spread strictly increases after dissolution completes) is a robust, test-asserted fact on top. A representative run: RMS spread keeps strictly increasing after dissolution completes (~5.3 at tick 16 to ~7.6 at tick 24, an 8-tick post-dissolution window) -- genuine scattering, not particles piling up against a wall or the field boundary. The whole arc -- hold, cross, resorb, scatter -- is code-verified simulation output from one coupling process plus one seeded movement process. The trigger timing is simulation-derived (the footprint-local acetate crossing the viability bound), but the resorption ramp itself is SCRIPTED (resorb_per_tick=6.0), not an emergent dissolution rate; with the scatter now seeded, the whole arc is reproducible run to run, magnitude and direction alike. This is precisely the failure cascade the paper's §Disintegration describes: once viability is lost, energy production can no longer sustain transport, membranes lose integrity, gradients collapse, and the system transitions from an actively maintained, far-from-equilibrium state to one dominated by diffusion, equilibration, and mechanical relaxation. The two phases of the run read directly as that transition: while the cell holds, an actively maintained CPM domain resists the rising acetate; once released, the shed material is handed to `BrownianMovement`, and the scattering debris cloud IS the diffusion-dominated, equilibrating regime -- the far-from-equilibrium cell relaxing toward equilibrium as literal drifting particles. As the paper notes, most models do not represent this transition explicitly: agent-based models remove cells mid-simulation, and whole-cell models assume viability -- so a spatial model that carries a coherent domain all the way through its own dissolution into diffusing debris is the point.

Result

Over a 20-24 tick run, the CPM cell holds coherent (area > 30) through tick 6, the footprint-local acetate mean crosses viability_threshold at released_tick == 7, the domain resorbs to area 0 by tick 16, and sheds exactly 63 particles into a shared debris store -- the CPM trajectory (hold/cross/resorb, released_tick, area, shed particle count) is fixed-seed deterministic and reproduces exactly run to run. The CPM->particle mass ledger closes (63 shed == 63 distinct vacated pixels <= 69 unique vacated -- shed material, not deleted-then-recreated mass; building it caught and fixed a real 6-pixel double-count). That debris cloud's RMS spread keeps strictly increasing through tick 24 (~5.3 -> ~7.6 in a representative run) -- genuine scattering, not wall-saturation; BrownianMovement is unseeded, so the exact RMS magnitudes vary run-to-run and are representative rather than reproducible, but the strict increase is the robust, test-asserted claim. The paper's §Disintegration level-shift (Fig 5) holds as spatial dissolution with a scripted resorption ramp (resorb_per_tick=6.0) -- the trigger timing is simulation-derived, the resorption schedule is scripted -- composed from independent frameworks (viva-cpm + spatio-flux particles) through one coupling process plus one stock movement process. The stressor here is literally `acetate`, the SAME molecule that is metabolic waste and cross-feeding food in `cell-cell-coupling` -- one molecule whose meaning is set relationally by each cell's interface (§Cell–cell coupling): nutrient there, toxin here. And the scatter phase reads directly as §Disintegration's failure cascade -- the actively maintained, far-from-equilibrium cell relaxing, once viability is lost, into a diffusion-dominated debris cloud, the transition most models skip (agent-based models delete the cell, whole-cell models assume viability).

Conclusion logic — gate decision

✓ If primary tests pass:
Implementation: disintegration-spatial.composite.json builds and runs to completion through the engine over a deterministic 20-24 tick run.
Empirical validation: The spatial §Disintegration level-shift pattern (Fig 5) is demonstrated at the toy-real (uncalibrated) level, with genuine per-pixel CPM shape dynamics, a real diffusing acetate field, and a verified particle bridge behind the hold-cross-resorb-scatter arc, rather than a scripted trajectory.
✗ If primary tests fail:
Diagnose:
  • the cell fails to hold coherent before crossing (the acetate gradient is too aggressive, or the cell's viability threshold is being crossed spuriously at the seed footprint)
  • released_tick drifts or the released flag never latches (the diffusion coefficient or gradient shape no longer brings the footprint-local mean above threshold within the run)
  • the domain fails to resorb to 0 (resorb_per_tick too small relative to the run length, or the target-volume ramp is not being applied after release)
  • shed particles are missing or the debris cloud stops scattering (the CPM-to-particle bridge is broken, or BrownianMovement is not wired to the particles store)
Block downstream: any future spatial study reusing the CpmDisintegration pattern or the CPM-to-particle bridge (e.g. a spatial growth-and-division study that needs to shed material on division)

Claims

  • Composing one real CPM cell with a real spatio-flux acetate field and a real spatio-flux particle-movement process through two coupling processes (CpmDisintegration for the viability-collapse trigger and resorption, stock BrownianMovement for the debris scatter) reproduces the paper's §Disintegration level-shift (Fig 5) as spatial dissolution with a scripted resorption ramp (resorb_per_tick=6.0) -- simulation-derived trigger timing, scripted resorption. Over a 20-24 tick run: the cell holds coherent (area > 30) through tick 6, the footprint-local acetate mean crosses viability_threshold at released_tick == 7, the domain resorbs to area 0 by tick 16, and sheds 63 particles -- the CPM trajectory (hold/cross/resorb, released_tick, area, shed particle count) is fixed-seed deterministic and reproduces exactly. The CPM->particle mass ledger closes -- 63 shed == 63 distinct vacated pixels <= 69 unique vacated, shed material not deleted-then-recreated mass (building it caught and fixed a real 6-pixel double-count; see limitations). The debris cloud's RMS spread keeps strictly increasing through tick 24 (~5.3 -> ~7.6 in a representative run); BrownianMovement is unseeded, so those exact magnitudes vary run-to-run -- only the strict increase is the robust, reproducible claim.
  • The stressor is literally acetate -- the SAME molecule that is metabolic waste and cross-feeding food in cell-cell-coupling, here a toxin whose high local concentration crosses the cell's viability bound.   • The two phases realize §Disintegration's failure cascade end to end: an actively maintained, far-from-equilibrium CPM domain, once viability is lost, relaxes into a diffusion-dominated, equilibrating debris cloud under BrownianMovement. Most models skip this transition explicitly -- agent-based models remove cells mid-simulation and whole-cell models assume viability -- so carrying a coherent domain through its own dissolution into diffusing debris is the explanatory gain.

Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • The disintegration mechanism realized here is **resorption + particle-shedding**, not in-lattice fragmentation. In this `viva-cpm` build the connectivity/"E1" structural-integrity constraint is a verified no-op (ON and OFF give statistically indistinguishable fragmentation under fragmenting energetics, and toggling it mid-run changes nothing -- a verified engine limitation), and `lambda_volume` / `temperature` are init-only with no runtime setters, so a settled CPM domain cannot be shattered into scattered pixels in-lattice. The code-verified route taken here is to resorb the cell (`set_target_volume -> 0`) and convert its shed pixels into particles via a separate, verified bridge. This is a chosen realization of "the coherent domain dissolves," not emergent lattice fragmentation -- a real engine limitation.
  • Debris particles move under spatio-flux `BrownianMovement` -- NumPy diffusion over a bounded grid -- not rigid-body pymunk physics. The shed particles drift and scatter; they do not collide with each other, with the dissolving cell, or fall under gravity.
  • The acetate field's radial gradient (peak 1.5, ring onset at radius 5, saturating at radius 18), `viability_threshold` (0.5), `resorb_per_tick` (6.0), `max_particles_per_tick` (8), and the diffusion coefficients (acetate D=4.0, debris diffusion_rate=1.0) are toy-real: plausible, hand-tuned magnitudes chosen to make hold -> cross -> resorb -> scatter all legible within a 20-tick run, not fitted to a named stressor, material, or literature target.
  • Unlike studies 2-3 in this investigation (`cell-environment-coupling`, `cell-cell-coupling`), this study's CPM cell has NO metabolism/cobra path at all -- `CpmDisintegration` is deliberately built around the viability-collapse trigger alone, not nutrient uptake or growth.
  • The `released` flag LATCHES once the crossing happens and never un-crosses, even once the footprint empties and its mean-stressor reading would otherwise read 0.0 -- a deliberate design choice so an emptied, dissolved cell cannot spuriously "recover" viability.
  • The CPM-to-particle bridge (a process emitting particles via the map `_add` sentinel into a shared `map[particle]` store, moved by a separate stock `BrownianMovement` process) is no longer an assumption; it is runtime-verified directly (`tests/test_particle_bridge_spike.py`) against a synthetic emitter, independently of the disintegration mechanism itself.

  • The CPM->particle mass ledger is CLOSED (answers peer-review issue M5, previously PENDING). An external ledger -- reading only the CPM lattice via world.snapshot(), never the process's private state nor the movement-scrambled particle positions -- replays the footprint diff vacated = prev_fp & ~curr_fp tick by tick. Building it caught a REAL double-count: the pre-fix shedding (min(vacated, cap), no memory) shed 68 particles over only 62 distinct pixels, because a pixel can be vacated, re-occupied by ordinary CPM fluctuation as the footprint drifts while resorbing, then vacated again -- and was shed a second time at the same pixel center (6 such re-vacated pixels = 6 units of manufactured mass, exactly the 68-vs-56 gap M5 flagged). Note the weak bound shed(68) <= unique_vacated(69) held even for the buggy version, which is why it masked the bug -- only shed == distinct-pixels exposes it. Fixed with an already-shed-pixel guard (cap now counts FRESH emissions): the process sheds 63 particles at 63 distinct pixels, equal to the live particle-store growth and <= 69 unique vacated pixels. The ledger closes -- shed material, not deleted-then-recreated mass. The 63-vs-56px gap is legitimate footprint drift: the cell traverses 69 distinct lattice pixels over its released lifetime, not the 56 held at the release instant. The remaining 69-63 = 6 gap is the per-tick cap dropping the steepest vacation ticks (vacated-but-unshed, the safe under-counting direction), never mass double-counted (tests/test_disintegration_ledger.py, tests/test_disintegration_mass.py).
  • Read against a lumped, specification-stage realization of the same pattern: that kind of model closes the paper's §Disintegration level-shift (Fig 5) with a scripted viability scalar decaying on a thermal-shock timer, over a lumped cell-state composite. This study closes the same level-shift with a real CPM cell's structural viability collapsing under a real diffusing acetate field, its domain genuinely resorbing in lattice pixels rather than a scalar dropping toward zero, and its shed material becoming literal scattering particles rather than an abstract debris variable. Compare the two for the same disintegration claim at two very different mechanistic resolutions.
